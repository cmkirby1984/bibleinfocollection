# Greek & Hebrew Word Study Guide

Understanding the Bible's original languages (without learning Greek or Hebrew!)

---

## Introduction

**"All Scripture is God-breathed..." - 2 Timothy 3:16**

The Bible was written in three languages:
- **Hebrew** - Most of the Old Testament
- **Aramaic** - Small portions of OT (Daniel, Ezra)
- **Greek** - The entire New Testament

**You don't need to learn Greek or Hebrew to study the Bible deeply.** But understanding key words and concepts from the original languages can:
- Reveal nuances lost in translation
- Clarify difficult passages
- Deepen understanding of God's character
- Enrich worship and prayer

**This guide shows you how to do meaningful word studies using free tools, even if you've never studied the languages.**

## Why Study Original Languages?

### Reason 1: Translation Always Involves Interpretation

**No translation is perfect.**
- Hebrew/Greek words don't always have exact English equivalents
- Context determines meaning
- Translators must choose between multiple valid options

**Example:**
- Greek word **agape** = love (but specific type: sacrificial, unconditional love)
- English "love" can mean many things (I love pizza vs. I love my spouse)
- Knowing Greek word clarifies what kind of love

---

### Reason 2: Words Have Ranges of Meaning

**One word can mean different things in different contexts.**

**Example: Greek *logos* (λόγος)**
- Word (John 1:1)
- Speech/message (Acts 14:12)
- Reason/account (Acts 19:40)
- Teaching (Acts 18:11)

**Understanding the range helps you see which meaning fits the context.**

---

### Reason 3: Some Concepts Don't Translate Well

**Hebrew/Greek thinking differs from English thinking.**

**Example: Hebrew *shalom* (שָׁלוֹם)**
- English: "peace" (absence of conflict)
- Hebrew: wholeness, completeness, well-being, prosperity, harmony with God
- Much richer than English "peace"

---

### Reason 4: Uncovers Word Plays and Patterns

**Original languages reveal patterns invisible in English.**

**Example: John 1:1-5**
- Greek word *logos* (Word) used repeatedly
- Creates intentional pattern/emphasis
- English translations might vary the word, losing the connection

---

### What You DON'T Need to Do

**You don't need to:**
- Learn to read Hebrew or Greek fluently
- Memorize vocabulary lists
- Understand complex grammar
- Spend years in seminary

**You DO need:**
- Free online tools (Strong's Concordance, Blue Letter Bible, etc.)
- This guide
- 15-30 minutes per word study
- Curiosity!

## Hebrew Basics (Old Testament)

### Characteristics of Biblical Hebrew

**1. Read right to left** (opposite of English)

**2. Only consonants written originally** (vowels added later by Masoretes ~600-1000 AD)

**3. Root system**
- Most words built from 3-letter roots
- Roots carry core meaning
- Different forms express different aspects

**Example:**
- Root: **KTB** (כתב) = "write"
- **katav** = he wrote
- **katub** = written
- **miktav** = letter/writing
- **sofer** = scribe (one who writes)

**4. Verb-focused** - Action and relationship emphasized over abstract concepts

**5. Concrete imagery** - Uses physical metaphors for spiritual truths
- "God is my rock" (strength, stability)
- "Wash me" (cleansing from sin)

---

### Hebrew Thought vs. Greek Thought

| Hebrew Thinking | Greek Thinking |
|-----------------|----------------|
| Concrete, physical | Abstract, philosophical |
| Action, relationship | Being, essence |
| Metaphor, story | Logic, systematics |
| "Show me" | "Explain to me" |

**Example:**
- **Hebrew:** "The LORD is my shepherd" (Psalm 23:1) - concrete image
- **Greek:** "God is love" (1 John 4:8) - abstract definition

---

### Important Hebrew Grammar Features

**1. Hebrew has no past/present/future tenses (like English)**
- Instead: completed action vs. incomplete action
- Changes how we understand prophecy and narrative

**2. Word order is flexible**
- Emphasis shown by placement, not just words used

**3. Parallelism in poetry**
- Second line repeats or contrasts first line
- Reveals meaning through comparison

**Example: Psalm 19:1**
- "The heavens declare the glory of God"
- "the skies proclaim the work of his hands"
- Same idea, different words = emphasis

---

## Greek Basics (New Testament)

### Characteristics of Biblical Greek (Koine Greek)

**1. Read left to right** (like English)

**2. Koine (κοινή) = "common"**
- Common language of Roman Empire
- Not classical Greek (Homer, Plato)
- Everyday language of the street

**Why this matters:** NT was written in language common people could understand (not elite scholarly language)

**3. Precise and nuanced**
- More verb tenses than English
- Distinctions English can't capture

**4. Alphabet:**
24 letters (α, β, γ, δ, ε, ζ, η, θ, ι, κ, λ, μ, ν, ξ, ο, π, ρ, σ, τ, υ, φ, χ, ψ, ω)

---

### Important Greek Grammar Features

**1. Verb Tenses**

More than English past/present/future:
- **Aorist** - Simple action (did something)
- **Present** - Continuous action (doing something)
- **Perfect** - Completed action with ongoing effects (has done, still true)
- **Imperfect** - Continuous action in past (was doing)
- **Future** - Will do
- **Pluperfect** - Had done (rare)

**Why it matters:**

**John 19:30 - "It is finished"**
- Greek: *tetelestai* (τετέλεσται) - perfect tense
- Not just "I finished" but "It is finished AND remains finished forever"
- Jesus' work on the cross is complete and permanently effective

**2. Multiple Words for "Love"**

English has one word: "love"
Greek has four:
- **Agape** (ἀγάπη) - Sacrificial, unconditional love
- **Philia** (φιλία) - Friendship love
- **Storge** (στοργή) - Family affection (not used in NT)
- **Eros** (ἔρως) - Romantic love (not used in NT)

**John 21:15-17:** Jesus asks Peter "Do you agape me?" Peter responds "I phileo you" (in Greek) - subtle distinction

**3. Definite Article ("the")**

Greek uses "the" more specifically than English
- Presence or absence can change meaning

**Example: Ephesians 2:8**
"By grace you have been saved through THE faith"
- Greek has definite article (missing in some English translations)
- Not just any faith, but THE faith (the gospel, Christian faith)

## Essential Hebrew Words

### 1. **YHWH / Yahweh / LORD** (יהוה)

**Meaning:** God's personal covenant name, revealed to Moses

**Significance:**
- Most sacred name
- Jews wouldn't pronounce it (said *Adonai* instead)
- English translations: LORD (all caps)
- Related to "I AM WHO I AM" (Exodus 3:14)

**Usage:** ~6,800 times in OT

**When you see "LORD":** Remember this is God's personal, covenant name (not just a title)

---

### 2. **Elohim** (אֱלֹהִים)

**Meaning:** God, gods (plural form, but used with singular verb for one true God)

**Significance:**
- General word for deity
- Used for the one true God and false gods
- Plural form may hint at Trinity

**First word for God in Bible:** "In the beginning Elohim created..." (Genesis 1:1)

---

### 3. **Hesed** (חֶסֶד)

**Translation:** Steadfast love, lovingkindness, mercy, faithful love

**Meaning:** Loyal, covenant love; God's unfailing commitment to His people

**Significance:**
- Key characteristic of God
- More than emotion - includes action, faithfulness, loyalty
- Covenant love that never gives up

**Example: Psalm 136** - "His hesed endures forever" (repeated 26 times)

---

### 4. **Shalom** (שָׁלוֹם)

**Translation:** Peace

**Meaning:** Wholeness, completeness, well-being, prosperity, harmony, nothing missing, nothing broken

**Significance:**
- More than absence of war
- Total well-being in all areas (spiritual, physical, emotional, relational)
- What God desires for His creation

**Usage:** Greeting (Hello/Goodbye), blessing, description of God's kingdom

---

### 5. **Shema** (שְׁמַע)

**Meaning:** Hear

**Significance:**
- First word of Judaism's most important prayer (Deuteronomy 6:4)
- "Shema, Israel: YHWH our Elohim, YHWH is one"
- Not just auditory hearing, but obedient listening

**Hebrew "hear" includes:** Listen, understand, obey, respond

---

### 6. **Ruach** (רוּחַ)

**Translation:** Spirit, wind, breath

**Meaning:** Wind, breath, spirit (same word for all three)

**Significance:**
- God's Spirit (ruach)
- Breath of life
- Wind of God
- Cannot separate spirit from breath/wind in Hebrew thinking

**Example:** Genesis 1:2 - "Spirit of God was hovering" (ruach Elohim)

---

### 7. **Chesed** - See *Hesed* (alternate spelling)

---

### 8. **Kavod** (כָּבוֹד)

**Translation:** Glory, weight, honor

**Literal meaning:** Heavy, weighty

**Significance:**
- God's glory = His "weightiness," importance, majesty
- Something substantial, not ethereal
- Worthy of honor because of substance/reality

**Example:** "The glory (kavod) of the LORD filled the temple" (1 Kings 8:11)

---

### 9. **Torah** (תּוֹרָה)

**Translation:** Law

**Meaning:** Instruction, teaching, direction

**Significance:**
- Not just rules/regulations
- God's loving instruction for life
- The first five books of the Bible

**Better understanding:** When Psalm 119 says "I delight in your torah," it's not legalism - it's delight in God's teaching/guidance

---

### 10. **Chesed V'emet** (חֶסֶד וֶאֱמֶת)

**Translation:** Steadfast love and faithfulness

**Significance:**
- Paired repeatedly in OT
- Describes God's character
- Hesed = loyal love; Emet = faithfulness/truth
- God's love is faithful; His faithfulness is loving

**Example:** Exodus 34:6 - "The LORD, the LORD, the compassionate and gracious God, slow to anger, abounding in chesed v'emet"

## Essential Greek Words

### 1. **Agape** (ἀγάπη)

**Meaning:** Unconditional, sacrificial love

**Significance:**
- God's kind of love
- Not based on feelings or worthiness of object
- Chooses to love regardless of response

**Examples:**
- John 3:16 - "God so agaped the world"
- 1 John 4:8 - "God is agape"
- 1 Corinthians 13 - Love (agape) chapter

---

### 2. **Charis** (χάρις)

**Meaning:** Grace, favor, gift

**Significance:**
- Unearned favor
- Gift given to undeserving
- Foundation of salvation

**Ephesians 2:8** - "By charis you have been saved through faith"

**Related:** *Charisma* (χάρισμα) = gift, especially spiritual gift

---

### 3. **Pistis** (πίστις)

**Meaning:** Faith, belief, trust

**Significance:**
- Not just intellectual agreement
- Trust, reliance, commitment
- Verb form (*pisteuo*) = believe, trust, entrust oneself

**Example:** Romans 1:17 - "The righteous will live by pistis"

---

### 4. **Logos** (λόγος)

**Translation:** Word, message, reason

**Meaning:**
- Word spoken
- Message/teaching
- Reason/logic
- Divine revelation

**Significance:**
- **John 1:1** - "In the beginning was the Logos... the Logos was God"
- Jesus is God's ultimate communication/revelation
- All of God's promises, purposes, and revelation embodied in Christ

---

### 5. **Dunamis** (δύναμις)

**Translation:** Power

**Meaning:** Power, might, strength, ability, miracle

**Significance:**
- Inherent power (not authority)
- Miraculous power
- Root of English "dynamite"

**Examples:**
- Acts 1:8 - "You will receive dunamis when the Holy Spirit comes"
- Romans 1:16 - "Gospel is the dunamis of God for salvation"

---

### 6. **Exousia** (ἐξουσία)

**Translation:** Authority

**Meaning:** Right to act, authority, jurisdiction, power

**Significance:**
- Delegated authority (vs. dunamis = inherent power)
- Right to exercise power
- Jesus' authority over demons, disease, death

**Example:** Matthew 28:18 - "All exousia in heaven and earth has been given to me"

---

### 7. **Metanoia** (μετάνοια)

**Translation:** Repentance

**Meaning:** Change of mind, transformation of thinking

**Literally:** *Meta* (after, change) + *nous* (mind) = "after-mind" or "changed mind"

**Significance:**
- Not just feeling sorry
- Fundamental change in thinking leading to change in behavior
- Turning from sin to God

**Example:** Mark 1:15 - "Metanoia and believe the gospel"

---

### 8. **Koinonia** (κοινωνία)

**Translation:** Fellowship, communion, participation, sharing

**Meaning:** Partnership, close association, sharing in common

**Significance:**
- Deep relationship, not just socializing
- Sharing life together
- Participation in Christ and with one another

**Example:** Acts 2:42 - "They devoted themselves... to the koinonia"

---

### 9. **Soteria** (σωτηρία)

**Translation:** Salvation, deliverance

**Meaning:** Rescue, deliverance, preservation, salvation

**Significance:**
- Holistic salvation: past (justified), present (sanctified), future (glorified)
- Rescue from sin's penalty, power, and presence

**Example:** Philippians 2:12 - "Work out your soteria with fear and trembling"

---

### 10. **Ekklesia** (ἐκκλησία)

**Translation:** Church

**Meaning:** Called-out ones, assembly

**Literally:** *Ek* (out) + *kaleo* (call) = "called out"

**Significance:**
- Not a building
- People called out from the world to belong to God
- Assembly of believers

**Example:** Matthew 16:18 - "I will build my ekklesia"

---

### 11. **Doxa** (δόξα)

**Translation:** Glory

**Meaning:** Glory, splendor, majesty, praise

**Significance:**
- God's manifest presence and character
- Glory given to God (praise, honor)
- Glory reflected by believers

**Example:** John 1:14 - "We have seen his doxa, doxa as of the only Son from the Father"

---

### 12. **Makarios** (μακάριος)

**Translation:** Blessed

**Meaning:** Blessed, happy, fortunate

**Significance:**
- Deep, abiding well-being (not temporary happiness)
- Blessedness that comes from God
- Used in the Beatitudes

**Example:** Matthew 5:3-11 - "Makarios are the poor in spirit..."

## How to Do a Word Study

### Step-by-Step Process

#### Step 1: Choose a Significant Word

**Look for:**
- Words repeated multiple times
- Theologically important words (love, grace, faith, etc.)
- Words you don't fully understand
- Key words in a passage you're studying

**Example:** Let's study *agape* (love)

---

#### Step 2: Find the Original Language Word

**Use an interlinear Bible or online tool:**
- Blue Letter Bible (blueletterbible.org)
- Bible Hub (biblehub.com)
- YouVersion (with Strong's numbers)

**Look for Strong's Number:**
- System that numbers every Hebrew/Greek word in Bible
- Example: *Agape* = Strong's G26

---

#### Step 3: Identify the Word's Range of Meaning

**Look up the Strong's definition:**
- Primary meaning
- Related meanings
- How it's used in classical Greek (if relevant)

**Example: *Agape* (G26)**
- Love, especially God's love
- Feast of charity
- Dear, beloved

---

#### Step 4: Study Every Occurrence (or Representative Sample)

**For common words:**
- Too many occurrences to study all
- Look at 10-20 representative uses

**For less common words:**
- Study every occurrence
- See how context shapes meaning

**Questions to ask:**
- Who is doing the action?
- To whom?
- In what context?
- What's the result?

---

#### Step 5: Look for Patterns and Themes

**Ask:**
- How does the OT use this concept?
- How does the NT develop it?
- Does Jesus redefine or emphasize it?
- What does it reveal about God's character?

**Example: Love (Agape)**
- OT: Love of God, love for neighbor
- NT: Defined by Jesus' sacrifice (John 3:16, 1 John 4:10)
- Command to love one another as Christ loved us

---

#### Step 6: Apply What You've Learned

**Ask:**
- How does this deeper understanding change how I read the passage?
- What does it reveal about God?
- How should it affect my life?
- What lies does this truth combat?

---

## Example Word Studies

### Example 1: "Propitiation" (Romans 3:25)

**Step 1: Identify the word**
- Romans 3:25 - "whom God put forward as a propitiation"
- Greek: *hilasterion* (ἱλαστήριον)
- Strong's G2435

**Step 2: Range of meaning**
- Mercy seat (lid of ark of covenant where blood was sprinkled)
- Propitiation (sacrifice that turns away wrath)
- Atoning sacrifice

**Step 3: Study key occurrences**
- **Romans 3:25** - Christ is the hilasterion
- **Hebrews 9:5** - Cherubim overshadowing the mercy seat (hilasterion)
- **1 John 2:2** - Christ is the hilasmos (related word) for our sins

**Step 4: OT background**
- Mercy seat in the Tabernacle/Temple
- Where high priest sprinkled blood on Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16)
- Where God's presence met humans
- Blood covered sins, satisfied God's justice

**Step 5: Application**
- Jesus is the New Testament "mercy seat"
- His blood satisfies God's justice
- Wrath turned away from us to Him
- God's presence now accessible through Christ

---

### Example 2: "Witness" (Acts 1:8)

**Step 1: Identify the word**
- Acts 1:8 - "You will be my witnesses"
- Greek: *martys* (μάρτυς)
- Strong's G3144

**Step 2: Range of meaning**
- Witness (one who testifies)
- Martyr (one who dies for testimony)

**Step 3: Related words**
- *Martyria* (μαρτυρία) - testimony, witness
- *Martyreo* (μαρτυρέω) - to bear witness, testify

**Step 4: Study key occurrences**
- **Acts 1:8** - You will be my witnesses
- **Acts 22:20** - "Blood of Stephen your witness (martys) was shed"
- **Revelation 2:13** - "Antipas, my faithful witness (martys), who was killed"
- **Revelation 11:3** - "I will grant authority to my two witnesses (martyrs)"

**Step 5: Pattern**
- Early Christians witnessed with their words
- Sometimes witnessed with their deaths
- Word "martyr" came from "witness"
- True witness is costly

**Step 6: Application**
- Being a witness may cost something
- Jesus asks for our testimony, whether easy or hard
- Ultimate witness is laying down life (literally or figuratively)

---

### Example 3: "Justified" (Romans 5:1)

**Step 1: Identify**
- "Having been justified by faith"
- Greek: *dikaioo* (δικαιόω)
- Strong's G1344

**Step 2: Range of meaning**
- To declare righteous (legal term)
- To show to be righteous
- To justify

**Step 3: Related words**
- *Dikaios* (δίκαιος) - righteous, just
- *Dikaiosyne* (δικαιοσύνη) - righteousness
- *Dikaioma* (δικαίωμα) - righteous act, justification

**Step 4: Key uses**
- **Romans 3:24** - Justified by his grace as a gift
- **Romans 4:5** - His faith is counted as righteousness (justification)
- **Romans 5:1** - Having been justified by faith
- **Romans 8:30** - Those he called he also justified

**Step 5: Background**
- Legal/courtroom term
- Not "make righteous" but "declare righteous"
- Forensic (legal) not transformational
- Judge's declaration, not process of change

**Step 6: Application**
- Justification = legal standing before God
- Declared "not guilty" because Christ took our guilt
- Righteousness credited to our account
- Past, completed action (perfect tense in Greek)

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Pitfall 1: The "Root Fallacy"

**Mistake:** Thinking the root word always determines meaning

**Example:**
- Greek *ekklesia* = "called out" (ek + kaleo)
- Doesn't mean every use emphasizes "called out"
- Context and usage determine meaning, not just etymology

**Better approach:** Consider etymology as one clue, but prioritize how the word is actually used

---

### Pitfall 2: Confusing All Uses of a Word

**Mistake:** Importing meaning from one context into another

**Example:**
- *Logos* means "word" in John 1:1
- But *logos* can also mean "account" (Luke 16:2)
- Don't assume "word" meaning in every occurrence

**Better approach:** Study each occurrence in its own context

---

### Pitfall 3: Over-Reliance on Strong's Numbers

**Mistake:** Thinking Strong's definition is complete

**Why it's a problem:**
- Strong's gives basic glosses, not full semantic range
- Doesn't explain grammar or syntax
- Can be outdated

**Better approach:** Use Strong's as a starting point; consult multiple resources

---

### Pitfall 4: Ignoring Grammar and Context

**Mistake:** Focusing only on vocabulary, ignoring grammar

**Example:**
- Verb tense matters (present vs. aorist)
- Word order matters
- Who is speaking? To whom? In what situation?

**Better approach:** Consider grammar and context alongside vocabulary

---

### Pitfall 5: "Original Language" Snobbery

**Mistake:** Thinking English translations are unreliable, only Greek/Hebrew is trustworthy

**Reality:**
- Good translations are reliable
- Committees of scholars produce modern translations
- Word studies enhance understanding but don't replace good translations

**Better approach:** Study original languages to deepen understanding, not to correct translators

---

### Pitfall 6: Parallelomania

**Mistake:** Seeing every Greek word as connected to Greek philosophy or culture

**Example:**
- *Logos* in John 1:1
- Some say John borrowed from Plato's philosophy
- But John's primary background is OT (Genesis 1, Wisdom literature)

**Better approach:** Prioritize biblical context over extrabiblical parallels

---

### Pitfall 7: Selective Use of Original Languages

**Mistake:** Only checking original languages when it supports your view

**Why it's a problem:**
- Confirmation bias
- Can miss correction to your theology

**Better approach:** Study words honestly, willing to have your view corrected

## Practical Applications

### 1. Enrich Your Prayer Life

**Use original language insights in prayer:**

**Example:**
- Knowing *agape* = unconditional love
- Pray: "Father, thank you for your agape love that chose me when I was unlovable"
- Knowing *hesed* = covenant faithfulness
- Pray: "LORD, your hesed never fails; you are faithful even when I'm faithless"

---

### 2. Deepen Worship

**Sing with understanding:**

**Example:**
- "Amazing grace" - *charis* = undeserved favor
- Understand the wonder of grace more deeply
- Worship from informed gratitude

---

### 3. Clarify Difficult Passages

**Word studies unlock meaning:**

**Example:**
- 1 Peter 3:21 - "Baptism now saves you"
- Greek *sozo* (save) has range: rescue, deliver, save, make whole
- Context: baptism as appeal to God (symbolic, not mechanical)
- Word study prevents misinterpretation

---

### 4. Teach More Accurately

**If you teach/lead Bible study:**
- Word studies add depth
- Clarify confusion
- Prevent error

**Example:**
Teaching on love? Explain *agape* vs. *phileo*. Changes how people understand "love one another."

---

### 5. Combat False Teaching

**Cults and false teachers often:**
- Twist Greek/Hebrew words
- Claim "the original language says..."
- Use root fallacy

**Being equipped helps you:**
- Check their claims
- See through manipulation
- Defend truth

---

### 6. Memorize with Greater Understanding

**When memorizing Scripture:**
- Study key words first
- Memorize with enriched understanding

**Example:**
- Memorizing Romans 8:1 - "No condemnation"
- Greek *katakrima* = judicial sentence against
- Memorize knowing it's a legal verdict: NOT GUILTY

**Choose one of these words:**
1. **Love** (agape) - 1 John 4:8
2. **Grace** (charis) - Ephesians 2:8
3. **Faith** (pistis) - Hebrews 11:1

**Steps:**

2. Look up the verse
3. Click "Tools" → "Interlinear"
4. Click the Greek word
5. Read Strong's definition
6. Click "Concordance" to see other uses
7. Read 5-10 other verses using that word
8. Write down what you learned

**Done! You've completed your first word study.**
