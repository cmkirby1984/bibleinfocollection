## What Is the Bible?

### The Basics

**Bible** = "books" (Greek: *biblia*)

**Two Testaments:**
- **Old Testament:** 39 books (Hebrew Scriptures)
- **New Testament:** 27 books (about Jesus and the early church)

**Total:** 66 books

**Written:**
- Over ~1,500 years
- By ~40 different authors (kings, fishermen, prophets, doctors, etc.)
- On 3 continents (Asia, Africa, Europe)
- In 3 languages (Hebrew, Aramaic, Greek)
- With one unified message: God's redemption through Jesus Christ

### Divine Authorship

**2 Timothy 3:16** - "All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness."

**2 Peter 1:20-21** - "No prophecy of Scripture came about by the prophet's own interpretation of things. For prophecy never had its origin in the human will, but prophets, though human, spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit."

**The Bible is:**
- **Inspired by God** - God-breathed
- **Written by humans** - Using their personalities, vocabularies, and circumstances
- **Without error** in the original manuscripts - Inerrant
- **Authoritative** - God's Word to us

## The Canon: Which Books?

**Canon** = "measuring rod" or "standard" - The books recognized as Scripture

### How Were Books Chosen?

**Not a random process!** Books weren't "chosen" by a church council. Rather, the church **recognized** which books God had inspired.

### Old Testament Canon

**The Hebrew Scriptures (39 books)**

**Jewish Division:**
1. **Torah (Law):** 5 books - Genesis to Deuteronomy
2. **Nevi'im (Prophets):** 8 books - Joshua, Judges, Samuel, Kings, Isaiah, Jeremiah, Ezekiel, The Twelve
3. **Ketuvim (Writings):** 11 books - Psalms, Proverbs, Job, etc.

**Total in Jewish Bible:** 24 books (same content as our 39, just divided differently)

**Jesus confirmed the OT canon:**
- **Luke 24:44** - "Everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms" (three divisions of Hebrew Bible)
- **Matthew 23:35** - Jesus referenced "from Abel to Zechariah" (first and last martyrs in Jewish Bible order)

**When was it recognized?**
- Jewish tradition: Completed by ~400 BC
- Council of Jamnia (~90 AD) affirmed books Jews already accepted
- Dead Sea Scrolls (150 BC - 70 AD) contain all OT books except Esther, confirming early recognition

### New Testament Canon

**27 Books**

**Criteria for Recognition:**

1. **Apostolic authorship or connection**
   - Written by an apostle OR
   - Close associate of an apostle (Mark with Peter, Luke with Paul)

2. **Early date**
   - Written in the first century
   - While eyewitnesses still alive

3. **Orthodox content**
   - Consistent with apostolic teaching
   - Confirmed gospel message

4. **Widespread acceptance**
   - Recognized and used by churches across the Roman Empire
   - Not just one region

5. **Divine qualities**
   - Spiritual power
   - Life-transforming message
   - Consistent with OT Scripture

### Timeline of NT Canon Recognition

**By 100 AD:**
- Four Gospels widely accepted
- Paul's letters collected and circulated
- Most NT books recognized

**~170 AD:**
- Muratorian Fragment lists 22/27 NT books
- Shows early wide agreement

**~200 AD:**
- Virtually all 27 books accepted
- A few disputed (Hebrews, James, 2 Peter, 2-3 John, Jude, Revelation)

**367 AD:**
- Athanasius' Easter letter lists exact 27 books
- First known document with our exact NT canon

**393 AD & 397 AD:**
- Councils of Hippo and Carthage formally affirmed the 27 books
- **Important:** They didn't CREATE the canon; they RECOGNIZED what the church already accepted

**Key Point:** The books were recognized as Scripture from the time they were written. Church councils simply affirmed what was already evident.

### What About the Apocrypha?

**Apocrypha** = 15 books written between the OT and NT (~400 BC - 100 AD)

**Examples:** 1-2 Maccabees, Tobit, Judith, Wisdom of Solomon, Ecclesiasticus, Baruch

**Protestant Position:**
- Not part of Hebrew Bible
- Not quoted as Scripture in NT
- Not considered inspired
- Helpful for history, but not canonical

**Catholic Position:**
- Included in their Bible (called "Deuterocanonical")
- Added by Council of Trent (1546)

**Why Protestants Reject Them:**
1. Not in Hebrew Bible
2. Never claimed to be inspired
3. Contains historical errors
4. Teaches doctrines contrary to canonical Scripture (prayers for the dead, purgatory)
5. Jesus and apostles never quoted them as Scripture

## Manuscript Evidence

### What Are Manuscripts?

**Manuscript** = Handwritten copy (no printing press until 1450s)

**The originals (autographs) no longer exist.** We have copies of copies. But the manuscript evidence is overwhelming.

### Old Testament Manuscripts

**Masoretic Text (MT)**
- Standard Hebrew text
- Copied by Masoretes (Jewish scribes) 500-1000 AD
- Extreme care in copying (counted every letter!)
- Oldest complete Hebrew Bible: Leningrad Codex (~1008 AD)

**Before 1947:**
- Oldest OT manuscript was ~1,000 years after originals
- Critics questioned accuracy

**Then came the Dead Sea Scrolls...**

### The Dead Sea Scrolls (1947)

**Discovery:** Shepherd boy found jars in caves near Qumran

**Contents:**
- ~900 manuscripts
- Dated 150 BC - 70 AD
- Every OT book except Esther
- **1,000 years older** than previous manuscripts!

**Result:**
- **99% identical** to Masoretic Text!
- Proved the OT was accurately preserved
- Showed scribes copied with extreme precision

**Example: Isaiah Scroll**
- Complete book of Isaiah
- Dated ~125 BC
- 1,000 years older than previous Isaiah manuscript
- Virtually identical to Masoretic Text

**Minor differences:** Mostly spelling variations, no doctrinal changes.

**Conclusion:** The OT we read today is what was written 2,000-3,000 years ago.

### New Testament Manuscripts

**The Numbers:**
- **5,800+ Greek NT manuscripts**
- **10,000+ Latin manuscripts**
- **9,300+ other language manuscripts**
- **Total: 25,000+ NT manuscripts!**

**Early Manuscripts:**
- **P52 (John Rylands Fragment):** ~125 AD - Contains John 18:31-33, 37-38
  - Only ~25-30 years after John was written!
- **P46 (Papyrus 46):** ~200 AD - Contains most of Paul's letters
- **P66, P75:** ~200 AD - Large portions of John
- **Codex Sinaiticus:** ~350 AD - Complete NT
- **Codex Vaticanus:** ~350 AD - Almost complete NT
- **Codex Alexandrinus:** ~400 AD - Complete NT

**Comparison to Other Ancient Works:**

| Work | Date Written | Earliest Copy | Time Gap | # of Copies |
|------|-------------|---------------|----------|-------------|
| **New Testament** | 50-100 AD | ~125 AD | **25-75 years** | **5,800+** |
| Homer's *Iliad* | ~800 BC | ~400 BC | 400 years | 1,800 |
| Caesar's *Gallic Wars* | ~50 BC | ~900 AD | 950 years | 10 |
| Plato | ~400 BC | ~900 AD | 1,300 years | 7 |
| Aristotle | ~340 BC | ~1,100 AD | 1,400 years | 5 |
| Tacitus *Annals* | ~100 AD | ~1,100 AD | 1,000 years | 20 |

**The NT is FAR better attested than any other ancient document!**

### Textual Variants

**Yes, there are differences between manuscripts.**

**Facts:**
- ~400,000 textual variants among NT manuscripts
- Sounds scary? It's not. Here's why:

**Why So Many?**
- Because we have SO MANY manuscripts!
- More manuscripts = more comparisons = more variants counted

**Nature of Variants:**
- **99% are spelling, word order, or scribal slips**
  - "Jesus Christ" vs "Christ Jesus"
  - "Lord" vs "the Lord"
- **No major doctrine affected**
- **We can reconstruct the original with 99.5% certainty**

**Textual criticism (comparing manuscripts) allows us to know the original text with greater confidence than any other ancient work.**

## How We Know the Bible Is Reliable

### 1. Manuscript Evidence (See Above)

**More manuscripts, earlier manuscripts, and more accurate copies than any other ancient document.**

### 2. Internal Consistency

**66 books, 40+ authors, 1,500 years, one unified message.**

- No contradictions in theology
- Progressive revelation (each part builds on previous)
- Fulfilled prophecy
- This is impossible without divine inspiration

### 3. Archaeological Confirmation

**Archaeology has repeatedly confirmed biblical accuracy:**

**Examples:**
- **Hittites:** Bible mentioned them; critics doubted. Archaeology proved they existed.
- **Pool of Bethesda (John 5:2):** Critics said it didn't exist. Archaeologists found it.
- **Pontius Pilate:** Extra-biblical confirmation found (Pilate Stone, 1961)
- **King David:** Tel Dan Inscription (1993) mentions "House of David"
- **Sodom and Gomorrah:** Evidence of destruction found near Dead Sea

**Nelson Glueck (archaeologist):** "No archaeological discovery has ever controverted a biblical reference."

### 4. Prophecy Fulfilled

**Old Testament contains 300+ prophecies about the Messiah.**

**Jesus fulfilled them all.** (See [Messianic Prophecies](../indexes/messianic-prophecies.md))

**Probability:** Mathematically impossible without divine foreknowledge.

**Other Prophecies:**
- Fall of Tyre (Ezekiel 26) - fulfilled in detail
- Babylonian captivity (Jeremiah 25:11) - 70 years, exactly
- Cyrus named 150 years before his birth (Isaiah 44:28; 45:1)
- Destruction of Jerusalem in 70 AD (Luke 21:20-24)

### 5. Changed Lives

**Hebrews 4:12** - "For the word of God is alive and powerful."

- Millions transformed by reading Scripture
- Consistent power to change lives for 2,000 years
- No other book has this impact

### 6. Jesus' Confirmation

**Jesus affirmed Scripture's authority:**
- **Matthew 5:18** - "Not the smallest letter, not the least stroke of a pen, will by any means disappear from the Law"
- **John 10:35** - "Scripture cannot be set aside"
- **Luke 24:44** - "Everything must be fulfilled that is written about me in the Law of Moses, the Prophets and the Psalms"

**If Jesus is God (and He is), and He affirmed Scripture, we can trust it completely.**

### 7. Early Church Witness

**Church Fathers (100-300 AD) quoted the NT extensively:**
- Could reconstruct almost entire NT from their quotations alone
- Shows NT books were widely known and accepted immediately

## The Septuagint (Greek Old Testament)

### What Is It?

**Septuagint (LXX)** = Greek translation of Hebrew OT

**When:** ~250-150 BC

**Why Important:**

1. **Shows OT canon was established by 250 BC**
2. **Used by Jesus and apostles**
   - Most NT quotations of OT are from Septuagint
3. **Spread Scripture throughout Greek-speaking world**
   - Prepared the world for the gospel
4. **Manuscript evidence**
   - Confirms Hebrew text accuracy

### The Legend

**"Seventy" translators** (hence "Septuagint") in Alexandria, Egypt translated Torah for Ptolemy II.

**Historical core:** Jewish scholars translated Hebrew Scriptures into Greek for diaspora Jews.

## Translation History

### Major English Translations Timeline

**1380s - Wycliffe Bible**
- First complete English Bible
- Translated from Latin Vulgate (not original languages)
- Handwritten (pre-printing press)
- Wycliffe persecuted for translating

**1526 - Tyndale's New Testament**
- William Tyndale
- First printed English NT
- Translated from Greek (used Erasmus' Greek text)
- 80% of KJV comes from Tyndale
- Tyndale burned at stake for his work (1536)

**1535 - Coverdale Bible**
- Miles Coverdale
- First complete printed English Bible

**1560 - Geneva Bible**
- Used by Pilgrims and Puritans
- First Bible with verse numbers
- Shakespeare used it

**1611 - King James Version (KJV)**
- Commissioned by King James I
- 47 scholars
- Based on Textus Receptus (Received Text)
- Majestic language
- Dominated for 300 years

**1881-1885 - Revised Version (RV)**
- Updated KJV language
- Used better Greek manuscripts (Westcott & Hort)

**1901 - American Standard Version (ASV)**
- American version of RV

**1952 - Revised Standard Version (RSV)**
- Updated ASV
- More readable

**1971 - New American Standard Bible (NASB)**
- Very literal translation
- Updated ASV
- Excellent for study

**1978 - New International Version (NIV)**
- Thought-for-thought translation
- Most popular modern translation
- Updated 1984, 2011

**1982 - New King James Version (NKJV)**
- Modernizes KJV language
- Retains KJV structure

**1996 - New Living Translation (NLT)**
- Very readable
- Thought-for-thought

**2001 - English Standard Version (ESV)**
- Revision of RSV
- Essentially literal
- Popular in evangelical churches

**2017 - Christian Standard Bible (CSB)**
- Optimal equivalence
- Balance of accuracy and readability

### Other Important Translations in History

**~405 AD - Latin Vulgate**
- By Jerome
- Latin translation from Hebrew and Greek
- Used by Catholic Church for 1,000+ years

**800s-1000s - Various language translations**
- Syriac, Coptic, Gothic, Armenian, Georgian, etc.
- Shows early spread of Scripture

## The Dead Sea Scrolls

### Why They Matter (Worth Repeating)

**Before 1947:**
- Skeptics said OT was changed over centuries
- Oldest manuscripts were from ~1000 AD

**After 1947:**
- Found manuscripts 1,000 years older
- Proved text was faithfully preserved
- Silenced critics

### What Was Found

**Location:** Qumran caves near Dead Sea

**Dates:** 150 BC - 70 AD

**Contents:**
- Biblical manuscripts (every OT book except Esther)
- Sectarian documents (community rules, etc.)
- Over 900 documents total

### The Isaiah Scroll

**Most famous discovery:**
- Complete book of Isaiah
- Dated ~125 BC
- **1,000 years older** than previous Isaiah manuscript

**Result:**
- Word-for-word identical to later manuscripts (with minor spelling variations)
- Proved scribes copied with incredible accuracy
- Confirmed prophecies about Christ were written BEFORE Christ

### Community at Qumran

**Who:** Likely Essenes (Jewish sect)
**When:** ~150 BC - 68 AD
**Why important:** Preserved these manuscripts

**Abandoned when Romans approached (66-70 AD)**
- Hid scrolls in jars in caves
- Forgotten for ~1,900 years
- Discovered by shepherd boy

**God's providence:** Preserved for us to find exactly when modern skepticism arose.

## Common Questions

### Q: Has the Bible been changed over the centuries?

**A:** No. Manuscript evidence proves remarkable consistency. While minor copyist errors exist, none affect doctrine. We can reconstruct the original text with 99.5% certainty.

---

### Q: What about lost books of the Bible?

**A:** Books referenced in the Bible (e.g., "Book of Jasher," "Chronicles of the Kings") were historical records, not Scripture. Only inspired books were preserved. Some "lost gospels" (Gospel of Thomas, etc.) were rejected because they were late (2nd-3rd century), heretical, and not apostolic.

---

### Q: Who decided which books to include?

**A:** God inspired specific books, and the church recognized them. The canon wasn't created by a council—it was recognized by believers from the beginning based on apostolic authority and divine qualities.

---

### Q: How do we know we have the right books?

**A:**
1. **Jesus affirmed the OT canon** (Luke 24:44)
2. **Apostolic authorship/connection for NT**
3. **Early church universal acceptance**
4. **Internal testimony of Holy Spirit** (believers recognize God's Word)
5. **Fulfilled prophecy and changed lives** prove divine origin

---

### Q: What about textual variants?

**A:** Yes, manuscripts have differences, but:
- 99% are insignificant (spelling, word order)
- No major doctrine affected
- We can determine original reading with high certainty
- Having many manuscripts helps us identify errors

---

### Q: Is the Bible corrupted?

**A:** No. This is a common Muslim claim, but:
- Manuscript evidence proves preservation
- Dead Sea Scrolls confirm OT accuracy
- 5,800+ NT Greek manuscripts show consistency
- God promised to preserve His Word (Isa 40:8; Matt 24:35)

---

### Q: Why are there different versions?

**A:** Because:
1. Original languages (Hebrew, Greek) need translating
2. Languages evolve (English today ≠ English in 1611)
3. Different translation philosophies (word-for-word vs thought-for-thought)
4. All major translations are faithful to original text

See [Translation Guide](translation-guide.md)

---

### Q: Can I trust my English Bible?

**A:** Yes! Modern English translations are based on:
- Thousands of manuscripts
- Centuries of scholarly work
- Original Hebrew and Greek texts

Choose a reliable translation (ESV, NIV, NASB, CSB, NLT) and trust it.
