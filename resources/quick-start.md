## I'm Brand New - Where Do I Start?

### START HERE

**Don't start at Genesis and try to read straight through!**

Here's why:
- The Bible isn't arranged chronologically
- Some books (Leviticus, Numbers) are difficult for beginners
- You'll likely get discouraged and quit

**Instead, start with the story of Jesus.**

---

### Your First Step: Gospel of John

**Read the Gospel of John first.**

**Why John?**
- Written specifically "that you may believe" (John 20:31)
- Tells the story of Jesus clearly
- Easier to understand than other Gospels
- Only 21 chapters - very doable

**How long?**
- 21 chapters = 1 chapter per day = 3 weeks
- Or read 2-3 chapters per day = 1 week

**What you'll learn:**
- Who Jesus is
- Why He came
- What He taught
- How to have eternal life

**Start tonight. Read John 1. You can do this.**

## First Week Plan

### Day-by-Day Reading for Your First Week

If you've never read the Bible, follow this simple plan:

**Day 1: John 1**
- Jesus is the Word who became flesh
- John the Baptist prepares the way
- First disciples follow Jesus

**Day 2: John 3**
- Nicodemus meets Jesus
- **John 3:16** - Most famous verse in the Bible
- "You must be born again"

**Day 3: John 4**
- Woman at the well
- Jesus offers "living water"
- True worship

**Day 4: John 6**
- Feeding of 5,000
- Jesus walks on water
- "I am the bread of life"

**Day 5: John 10**
- "I am the good shepherd"
- Jesus lays down His life for the sheep
- Security in Christ

**Day 6: John 14-15**
- "I am the way, the truth, and the life"
- The Holy Spirit promised
- "Remain in me"

**Day 7: John 19-20**
- Jesus' crucifixion
- Jesus' resurrection
- "My Lord and my God!"

**After one week, you'll have read the core of the gospel message.**

## First Month Plan

### Week 1: Gospel of John (21 chapters)
**Goal:** Understand who Jesus is

**Read:** 3 chapters per day = 7 days

**Key takeaway:** Jesus is God who became man to save us

---

### Week 2: Gospel of Mark (16 chapters)
**Goal:** See Jesus in action

**Read:** 2-3 chapters per day

**Why Mark next?**
- Shortest Gospel
- Fast-paced, action-packed
- Another angle on Jesus' life

**Key takeaway:** Jesus is the suffering Servant who came to serve and give His life

---

### Week 3: Acts (28 chapters)
**Goal:** See the early church in action

**Read:** 4 chapters per day

**Why Acts?**
- Continues the story after Jesus' resurrection
- Shows how the church began
- Exciting narrative

**Key takeaway:** The Holy Spirit empowers believers to spread the gospel

---

### Week 4: Romans or Ephesians
**Goal:** Understand core Christian doctrine

**Option A: Romans (16 chapters)**
- Deep theology
- Explains salvation clearly
- Paul's most systematic letter

**Option B: Ephesians (6 chapters)**
- Shorter, easier
- Who we are in Christ
- Practical Christian living

**Read:** Romans = 2 chapters per day; Ephesians = 1 chapter per day

**Key takeaway:** We're saved by grace through faith, not by works

---

### After One Month

**You'll have read:**
- Two Gospels
- Acts
- One letter explaining salvation

**You'll understand:**
- Who Jesus is
- Why He died
- How to be saved
- What the Christian life looks like

**Next steps:** See [First Year Plan](#first-year-plan) below

## Essential Books for Beginners

### Gospels - Start Here

**1. John** ⭐ START HERE
- Who Jesus is
- Signs and "I AM" statements
- Written to help you believe

**2. Mark** ⭐
- Shortest Gospel
- Action-packed
- Jesus the Servant

**3. Luke**
- Most detailed
- Jesus' compassion
- Great stories (Prodigal Son, Good Samaritan)

**4. Matthew**
- Jesus the King
- Sermon on the Mount
- Fulfillment of prophecy

**Read at least ONE Gospel first. John is best for beginners.**

---

### New Testament Letters - Core Theology

**5. Ephesians** ⭐
- Who you are in Christ
- 6 chapters, very manageable
- Practical and encouraging

**6. Philippians** ⭐
- Joy in Christ
- 4 chapters
- Paul's most personal letter

**7. 1 John** ⭐
- Assurance of salvation
- Love and obedience
- 5 chapters, easy to understand

**8. James**
- Practical Christian living
- Faith and works
- Wisdom for daily life

**9. Romans**
- Most systematic theology
- Deep but crucial
- Explains salvation clearly

---

### Old Testament - After You Know Jesus

**10. Genesis 1-11** ⭐
- Creation
- Fall
- Flood
- Foundation for everything

**11. Genesis 12-50**
- Abraham, Isaac, Jacob, Joseph
- God's covenant with His people

**12. Psalms** ⭐
- Worship and prayer
- Read one psalm per day
- Encouragement and comfort

**13. Proverbs** ⭐
- Practical wisdom
- One chapter per day (31 chapters = one month)
- Short, easy readings

**14. Daniel**
- Stories of faithfulness (lions' den, fiery furnace)
- Prophecy about Jesus
- Encouragement for difficult times

**15. Jonah**
- Short (4 chapters)
- God's mercy to Gentiles
- Foreshadows Jesus' resurrection

## Books to Save for Later

**Don't feel pressure to read these right away:**

### Old Testament Books for Later

**Leviticus** - Sacrificial laws; difficult for beginners
**Numbers** - Census and wilderness wandering; can be dry
**Deuteronomy** - Repetition of the law; important but challenging
**Chronicles** - Repeats Kings with genealogies
**Ezekiel** - Complex visions
**Minor Prophets** (except Jonah) - Need historical context

**NOTE:** These books are valuable! Just easier to understand after you know the big picture.

### New Testament Books for Later

**Hebrews** - Requires OT knowledge
**Revelation** - Highly symbolic; needs study helps

**You'll get to these eventually. Start with the essentials first.**

## How to Read the Bible

### Before You Read

**1. Pray**
- **Psalm 119:18** - "Open my eyes that I may see wonderful things in your law"
- Ask God to help you understand
- Ask the Holy Spirit to teach you

**2. Expect God to Speak**
- The Bible is living and active (Hebrews 4:12)
- God will speak to you through His Word

---

### As You Read

**3. Read Slowly**
- Don't rush
- Quality over quantity
- It's okay to read just a few verses if you're pondering them

**4. Ask Questions**
- **Observation:** What does it say?
- **Interpretation:** What does it mean?
- **Application:** What should I do?

**5. Mark Key Verses**
- Underline or highlight
- Make notes in the margin
- Use a journal

**6. Look for Jesus**
- **Luke 24:27** - All Scripture points to Christ
- How does this passage reveal God's character or plan of salvation?

---

### After You Read

**7. Reflect**
- What did I learn about God?
- What did I learn about myself?
- Is there a command to obey?
- Is there a promise to claim?
- Is there a sin to avoid?

**8. Pray Again**
- Thank God for what He showed you
- Ask for help to obey
- Pray Scripture back to God

**9. Apply**
- **James 1:22** - "Do not merely listen to the word... Do what it says"
- Pick one thing to apply today

## Common Beginner Questions

### Q: Do I need to read the Bible in order?

**A:** No! Start with the Gospels (especially John), then explore from there. The Bible is a library of books, not a single novel.

---

### Q: What if I don't understand something?

**A:** That's normal! Here's what to do:
1. Keep reading - Context often helps
2. Read it in another translation
3. Use study notes or a commentary
4. Ask a mature Christian
5. Don't get stuck - move on and come back later

**You don't have to understand everything to benefit from Scripture.**

---

### Q: How much should I read per day?

**A:** **Quality over quantity.**

**Good starting points:**
- 1 chapter per day
- 10-15 minutes
- One paragraph if that's all you can focus on

**It's better to read a little bit consistently than a lot sporadically.**

---

### Q: What translation should I use?

**A:** For beginners, try:
- **NLT** (New Living Translation) - Easiest to understand
- **NIV** (New International Version) - Clear and widely used
- **CSB** (Christian Standard Bible) - Good balance

See our [Translation Guide](translation-guide.md) for more details.

---

### Q: Should I take notes?

**A:** Yes! Writing helps you remember and process.

**Simple method:**
- Date
- What you read
- One thing that stood out
- One way to apply it

---

### Q: What if I miss a day?

**A:** Don't quit! Just pick up where you left off (or skip ahead if following a plan). Missing one day doesn't mean you failed.

**Grace, not guilt.**

---

### Q: How do I know what's applicable today vs. Old Testament law?

**A:** Great question!

**Old Testament Law had three categories:**
1. **Moral Law** (Ten Commandments, etc.) - Still applies
2. **Ceremonial Law** (Sacrifices, rituals) - Fulfilled in Christ
3. **Civil Law** (Israelite government) - For ancient Israel

**General principle:**
- If it's repeated in the New Testament, it applies to us
- Jesus fulfilled the ceremonial law (Hebrews 10:1-18)
- Principles from the law still teach us about God's character

**When in doubt, focus on the New Testament for Christian living.**

---

### Q: What about all the violence in the Old Testament?

**A:** This is a tough question. A few thoughts:
1. The OT records history - not everything recorded is endorsed
2. God's judgment is against real evil (child sacrifice, extreme wickedness)
3. God is both loving AND just
4. See our [Key Doctrines](key-doctrines.md) for more on God's justice

**Don't let difficult passages stop you from reading. Start with the New Testament and work backward.**

## Building a Daily Habit

### Keys to Success

**1. Same Time, Same Place**
- Pick a specific time (morning is best for most people)
- Pick a specific spot
- Have your Bible ready

**2. Start Small**
- 10 minutes per day
- One chapter
- Build from there

**3. Remove Distractions**
- Turn off phone/notifications
- Find a quiet spot
- Before you check social media

**4. Use a Reading Plan**
- Takes away "what should I read today?"
- Provides structure
- See [Reading Plans](reading-plans.md)

**5. Journal**
- Write what stands out
- Track what you're learning
- Review later to see growth

**6. Get Accountability**
- Tell someone you're starting
- Read with a friend
- Join a Bible study group

**7. Don't Be Legalistic**
- Missing a day ≠ failure
- God's not grading you
- Grace > guilt

---

### Sample Daily Routine

**Morning Bible Time (15 minutes):**

**5 minutes:** Pray - "God, speak to me through Your Word today"

**7 minutes:** Read - One chapter or passage

**3 minutes:** Reflect and journal:
- What did I learn about God?
- What should I do today?

**Prayer:** Ask God to help you apply what you learned

**Done!**
