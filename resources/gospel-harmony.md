# Gospel Harmony

Understanding the four Gospels and the life of Christ.

---

## Why Four Gospels?

**"Many have undertaken to draw up an account... so that you may know the certainty of the things you have been taught." - Luke 1:1, 4**

God gave us **four accounts** of Jesus' life, not one, because:

1. **Multiple witnesses establish truth** (Deuteronomy 19:15; 2 Corinthians 13:1)
2. **Different perspectives reveal a fuller picture** (like viewing a diamond from four angles)
3. **Each Gospel was written for a specific audience** with unique emphases
4. **Together they present the complete portrait of Christ**

The differences aren't contradictions—they're complementary perspectives that enrich our understanding.

## Matthew - The King

### Author & Audience
- **Author:** Matthew (Levi), a tax collector turned apostle
- **Audience:** Jewish readers
- **Purpose:** Prove Jesus is the long-awaited Messiah-King of Israel

### Key Characteristics

**1. Old Testament Focus**
- Contains ~60 quotations or allusions to the OT
- Repeatedly says "to fulfill what was spoken" (1:22; 2:15, 17, 23; 4:14; 8:17; 12:17; 13:35; 21:4; 27:9)
- Shows Jesus as the fulfillment of prophecy

**2. Jesus as King**
- Opens with genealogy from Abraham through David (royal line)
- Wise men seek "the king of the Jews" (2:2)
- Sermon on the Mount presents kingdom ethics (chapters 5-7)
- Parables of the kingdom (chapter 13)
- Triumphal entry as King (21:1-11)
- Crucified as "King of the Jews" (27:37)

**3. Teaching Emphasis**
- Five major teaching sections (like five books of Moses):
  1. Sermon on the Mount (5-7)
  2. Mission instructions (10)
  3. Kingdom parables (13)
  4. Church discipline (18)
  5. Olivet Discourse on end times (24-25)
- Each section ends with "when Jesus had finished" (7:28; 11:1; 13:53; 19:1; 26:1)

**4. Jewish Terminology**
- "Kingdom of heaven" (used 32 times, avoiding God's name out of reverence)
- References to Jewish customs without explanation (assumes knowledge)

**Unique to Matthew:**
- Visit of the Magi (2:1-12)
- Flight to Egypt (2:13-23)
- Peter walking on water (14:28-31)
- Temple tax (17:24-27)
- Judas' death (27:3-10)
- Guard at the tomb (27:62-66)
- Great Commission (28:18-20)

**Key Verse:**
**Matthew 1:1** - "This is the genealogy of Jesus the Messiah the son of David, the son of Abraham."

## Mark - The Servant

### Author & Audience
- **Author:** John Mark (Peter's companion; Acts 12:12, 25; 1 Peter 5:13)
- **Audience:** Roman readers
- **Purpose:** Present Jesus as the suffering Servant who came to serve and give His life

### Key Characteristics

**1. Action-Packed**
- Shortest Gospel (16 chapters)
- "Immediately" used 42 times (Greek: *euthus*)
- Fast-paced narrative—Jesus is always moving
- More about what Jesus DID than what He SAID

**2. Jesus as Servant**
- No genealogy (servants don't need pedigrees)
- No birth narrative
- Opens with John the Baptist preparing the way
- Key verse: "Even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many" (10:45)

**3. Emphasis on Suffering**
- Most detailed account of Jesus' suffering and crucifixion
- Peter's confession immediately followed by first passion prediction (8:27-38)
- Three passion predictions (8:31; 9:31; 10:33-34)
- Extended crucifixion narrative

**4. Attention to Detail**
- Includes vivid details (likely from Peter's eyewitness account)
- Emotional responses noted (1:41 "indignant"; 3:5 "anger and distress"; 10:21 "loved him")
- Specific numbers, times, places

**5. Explanations for Romans**
- Translates Aramaic phrases (3:17; 5:41; 7:11, 34; 15:22, 34)
- Explains Jewish customs (7:3-4; 15:42)
- Uses Latin terms (centurion, legion, praetorium)

**Unique to Mark:**
- Man with Legion (healing account is most detailed - 5:1-20)
- Deaf and mute man healed (7:31-37)
- Blind man at Bethsaida (8:22-26)
- Young man fleeing naked (14:51-52) - possibly Mark himself!

**Key Verse:**
**Mark 10:45** - "For even the Son of Man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."

## Luke - The Son of Man

### Author & Audience
- **Author:** Luke, a Gentile physician and Paul's companion (Colossians 4:14; 2 Timothy 4:11)
- **Audience:** Greek/Gentile readers (specifically Theophilus - Luke 1:3; Acts 1:1)
- **Purpose:** Present Jesus as the perfect Son of Man, Savior of all people

### Key Characteristics

**1. Careful Historian**
- Opens with historical prologue (1:1-4)
- "I myself have carefully investigated everything" (1:3)
- Most detailed birth narrative
- Dates events by rulers (2:1-2; 3:1-2)
- Links Jesus' life to world history

**2. Jesus as Son of Man**
- Genealogy traces back to Adam, father of all humanity (3:23-38)
- Universal salvation emphasized
- Jesus came to seek and save the LOST (19:10)

**3. Compassion and Inclusion**
- Highlights Jesus' compassion for:
  - Women (7:36-50; 8:1-3; 10:38-42; 13:10-17)
  - Samaritans (9:51-56; 10:25-37; 17:11-19)
  - Tax collectors and sinners (5:27-32; 7:36-50; 15:1-32; 19:1-10)
  - Poor and marginalized (4:18; 6:20; 14:12-14; 16:19-31)
  - Gentiles (2:32; 3:6, 38; 4:25-27; 7:9)

**4. Prayer and Worship**
- Jesus praying: 3:21; 5:16; 6:12; 9:18, 28-29; 11:1; 22:32, 41-45; 23:34, 46
- Songs of praise: Mary's Magnificat (1:46-55), Zechariah's Benedictus (1:68-79), Angels' Gloria (2:14), Simeon's Nunc Dimittis (2:29-32)
- Parables about prayer (11:5-13; 18:1-14)

**5. Joy and Celebration**
- Emphasis on joy and rejoicing (1:14, 44, 47; 2:10; 6:23; 10:20; 15:7, 10, 32; 24:52)
- Banquets and meals (5:29; 7:36; 10:38; 11:37; 14:1; 19:5; 22:14; 24:30, 41-43)

**6. The Holy Spirit**
- References to the Holy Spirit more than other Gospels
- 1:15, 35, 41, 67; 2:25-27; 3:16, 22; 4:1, 14, 18; 10:21; 11:13; 12:10, 12; 24:49

**Unique to Luke:**
- Birth announcements (1:5-80)
- Census and birth in manger (2:1-7)
- Shepherds (2:8-20)
- Jesus at age 12 in temple (2:41-52)
- Good Samaritan (10:25-37)
- Prodigal Son (15:11-32)
- Rich man and Lazarus (16:19-31)
- Ten lepers (17:11-19)
- Zacchaeus (19:1-10)
- Walk to Emmaus (24:13-35)

**Key Verse:**
**Luke 19:10** - "For the Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost."

## John - The Son of God

### Author & Audience
- **Author:** John, "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (13:23; 19:26; 20:2; 21:7, 20)
- **Audience:** Universal—written for all people, likely from Ephesus
- **Purpose:** "That you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name" (20:31)

### Key Characteristics

**1. Jesus' Deity**
- Opens with Jesus as the eternal Word/God (1:1-18)
- Seven "I AM" statements (claiming divine name from Exodus 3:14)
- Signs selected to prove Jesus is God (20:30-31)
- Climaxes with Thomas' declaration: "My Lord and my God!" (20:28)

**2. Theological Depth**
- Most theological/philosophical Gospel
- Abstract concepts: Word, Light, Life, Truth, Glory
- Long discourses (chapters 13-17)
- Reflective rather than narrative

**3. Seven Signs (Miracles)**
Selected to prove Jesus' identity:
1. Water to wine (2:1-11) - Glory revealed
2. Healing official's son (4:46-54) - Power over distance
3. Healing paralytic (5:1-15) - Power over disease
4. Feeding 5,000 (6:1-15) - Power over insufficiency
5. Walking on water (6:16-21) - Power over nature
6. Healing blind man (9:1-41) - Power over disability
7. Raising Lazarus (11:1-44) - Power over death

**4. Seven "I AM" Statements**
1. "I am the bread of life" (6:35)
2. "I am the light of the world" (8:12; 9:5)
3. "I am the door" (10:7, 9)
4. "I am the good shepherd" (10:11, 14)
5. "I am the resurrection and the life" (11:25)
6. "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (14:6)
7. "I am the true vine" (15:1, 5)

Plus absolute "I AM": John 8:58 - "Before Abraham was, I AM!"

**5. Unique Structure**
- **Book of Signs** (1-12) - Public ministry
- **Book of Glory** (13-21) - Private teaching and passion
- Upper Room Discourse (13-17) - Most intimate teaching
- High Priestly Prayer (17) - Jesus prays for all believers

**6. Key Words**
- "Believe" (98 times)
- "Life" (36 times)
- "Love" (44 times)
- "Truth" (25 times)
- "Witness/Testimony" (47 times)

**7. No Synoptic Parables**
- John records no parables from the Synoptics
- Instead: extended metaphors (shepherd, vine, etc.)

**8. Different Events Emphasized**
- Minimal miracle accounts (only 7 selected)
- Extended conversations: Nicodemus (3), Samaritan woman (4), Pool of Bethesda discourse (5), Bread of Life (6), Good Shepherd (10), etc.
- Three Passovers mentioned (2:13; 6:4; 11:55) - showing 3+ year ministry

**92% Unique Material:**
- Wedding at Cana (2:1-11)
- Nicodemus (3:1-21)
- Woman at the well (4:1-42)
- Entire Upper Room Discourse (13-17)
- Washing disciples' feet (13:1-17)
- High Priestly Prayer (17)
- Doubting Thomas (20:24-29)
- Breakfast by the sea (21:1-25)

**Key Verse:**
**John 20:31** - "But these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and that by believing you may have life in his name."

## The Synoptic Problem

### What Is It?

"Synoptic" means "seeing together." Matthew, Mark, and Luke are called the **Synoptic Gospels** because they share similar content, structure, and wording.

**The "problem":** How do we explain the similarities AND differences?

### Similarities
- ~90% of Mark appears in Matthew
- ~50% of Mark appears in Luke
- Matthew and Luke share material not in Mark (mostly teachings)
- Sometimes word-for-word identical in Greek

### Most Likely Explanation

**Markan Priority + Q Source:**

1. **Mark wrote first** (~55-65 AD)
   - Matthew and Luke both used Mark as a source
   - Explains the shared structure and wording

2. **"Q" source** (from German *Quelle* = "source")
   - A collection of Jesus' sayings (now lost)
   - Used by both Matthew and Luke
   - Explains shared material not in Mark (e.g., Lord's Prayer, Beatitudes, many parables)

3. **Unique sources (M and L)**
   - Matthew had additional sources ("M")
   - Luke had additional sources ("L")
   - Explains unique material in each

**Why It Matters:**
- Shows early Christians valued eyewitness testimony
- Multiple independent sources strengthen historical reliability
- God used different human authors with different sources while preserving truth

## Chronological Life of Christ

### The Big Picture (Approximate Timeline)

#### Early Life (~5 BC - AD 26)
1. **Pre-existence** (John 1:1-18)
2. **Announcements** (Luke 1)
3. **Birth** (~5-4 BC) (Matthew 1-2; Luke 2:1-20)
4. **Circumcision & dedication** (Luke 2:21-38)
5. **Magi visit** (Matthew 2:1-12)
6. **Flight to Egypt** (Matthew 2:13-23)
7. **Return to Nazareth** (Matthew 2:19-23; Luke 2:39)
8. **Boy Jesus in temple** (age 12) (Luke 2:41-52)
9. **Silent years** (~18 years - no biblical record)

#### Beginning of Ministry (AD 26-27)
10. **John the Baptist's ministry** (all four Gospels)
11. **Jesus' baptism** (Matthew 3; Mark 1; Luke 3; John 1)
12. **Temptation in wilderness** (Matthew 4; Mark 1; Luke 4)
13. **First disciples** (John 1:35-51)
14. **Wedding at Cana** (John 2:1-11)
15. **First temple cleansing** (John 2:13-25)
16. **Nicodemus** (John 3:1-21)
17. **Samaritan woman** (John 4:1-42)

#### Galilean Ministry (AD 27-29)
18. **Rejection at Nazareth** (Luke 4:16-30)
19. **Calling fishermen** (Matthew 4; Mark 1; Luke 5)
20. **Sermon on the Mount** (Matthew 5-7)
21. **Many miracles and teachings** (all Gospels)
22. **Choosing the Twelve** (Matthew 10; Mark 3; Luke 6)
23. **Kingdom parables** (Matthew 13; Mark 4; Luke 8)
24. **Feeding 5,000** (all four Gospels)
25. **Walking on water** (Matthew 14; Mark 6; John 6)
26. **Peter's confession** (Matthew 16; Mark 8; Luke 9)
27. **Transfiguration** (Matthew 17; Mark 9; Luke 9)

#### Later Judean & Perean Ministry (AD 29)
28. **Feast of Tabernacles** (John 7-8)
29. **Good Shepherd discourse** (John 10)
30. **Sending of the 72** (Luke 10)
31. **Raising Lazarus** (John 11)

#### Passion Week (AD 30)
32. **Triumphal Entry** - Sunday (all four Gospels)
33. **Temple cleansing** - Monday (Matthew 21; Mark 11; Luke 19)
34. **Teaching in temple** - Tuesday-Wednesday (all Gospels)
35. **Upper Room** - Thursday (all four Gospels)
36. **Gethsemane** - Thursday night (all four Gospels)
37. **Trials** - Thursday night/Friday morning (all four Gospels)
38. **Crucifixion** - Friday (all four Gospels)
39. **Burial** - Friday evening (all four Gospels)

#### Resurrection & Ascension (AD 30)
40. **Empty tomb** - Sunday (all four Gospels)
41. **Resurrection appearances** (all four Gospels, varying accounts)
42. **Great Commission** (Matthew 28; Mark 16)
43. **Ascension** - 40 days later (Luke 24; Acts 1)

## Why the Differences?

### Apparent Contradictions Explained

**1. Different Details ≠ Contradiction**
- Like four witnesses to a car accident—each notices different details
- All true, just different perspectives

**2. Theological Emphasis**
- Matthew emphasizes teaching → more sermons
- Mark emphasizes action → more miracles
- Luke emphasizes compassion → more stories of outcasts
- John emphasizes deity → more theological discourse

**3. Audience Needs**
- Jews needed proof of Messiahship (Matthew)
- Romans valued action and service (Mark)
- Greeks valued careful history (Luke)
- All people need to believe Jesus is God (John)

**4. Selectivity**
- John 21:25 - "Jesus did many other things... the whole world would not have room for the books"
- Each author selected material for his purpose

**5. Chronological Flexibility**
- Ancient biography didn't require strict chronology
- Sometimes grouped events by theme rather than time
- Luke 1:3 - "orderly account" doesn't necessarily mean chronological

## How to Study the Gospels

### 1. Read Each Gospel Individually
- Start with Mark (shortest, fastest-paced)
- Then Matthew, Luke, John
- Notice each Gospel's unique emphasis
- See [Bible Reading Plans](reading-plans.md)

### 2. Study Parallel Passages
- Read the same event in multiple Gospels
- Note what each Gospel includes/excludes
- Ask: Why did this author include this detail?

**Example: Peter's Confession (Matthew 16:13-20; Mark 8:27-30; Luke 9:18-21)**
- All three record Peter's confession: "You are the Christ"
- Only Matthew includes Jesus' response: "You are Peter, and on this rock..."
- Why? Matthew's emphasis on the church (16:18; 18:17—only Gospel using "church")

### 3. Study Unique Material
- What does this Gospel alone tell us?
- What would we miss without this account?

### 4. Trace Themes
- Pick a theme and follow it through all four Gospels
- Examples: Kingdom of God, faith, prayer, discipleship

### 5. Focus on Jesus
- What does this passage reveal about Jesus?
- How does it show His character, power, love, or deity?

### 6. Apply to Your Life
- How should this change how I live?
- What is God calling me to do/believe?

---

## Questions for Deeper Study

### About the Differences
1. Why does Matthew include the Magi but Luke includes the shepherds?
2. Why does John include long discourses that the Synoptics don't?
3. Why are Jesus' genealogies different in Matthew and Luke?
4. When did Jesus cleanse the temple—beginning (John 2) or end (Synoptics) of ministry? (Or twice?)

### About Each Gospel
1. What portrait of Jesus does each Gospel paint?
2. How would the original audience have understood this Gospel?
3. What unique contribution does this Gospel make?

### About Jesus
1. How do the four Gospels together give us a fuller picture of Christ?
2. What does this event/teaching reveal about Jesus' identity?
3. How does this fulfill Old Testament prophecy or typology?

---

## Conclusion

**Four Gospels = One Complete Portrait**

Like four angles of photography capturing a diamond:
- **Matthew** shows the King
- **Mark** shows the Servant
- **Luke** shows the perfect Man
- **John** shows the Son of God

Together they reveal:
- **Prophet** (teaching)
- **Priest** (sacrifice)
- **King** (authority)
- **God** (deity)

**Revelation 5:5-6** pictures Jesus as both:
- **Lion of Judah** (Matthew's King)
- **Lamb who was slain** (Mark's Servant, Luke's sacrifice, John's Lamb of God)

The differences aren't problems—they're providential. God gave us exactly what we need to know Jesus fully.
