# How to Share Your Faith: A Practical Guide to Evangelism

## Introduction

Every Christian has been given the incredible privilege and responsibility of sharing the good news of Jesus Christ. You don't need to be a pastor, evangelist, or Bible scholar to tell others about Jesus. If you've experienced God's grace, you have a story worth sharing.

This guide will equip you with practical tools, methods, and confidence to share your faith naturally in everyday conversations. Remember: God uses ordinary people to accomplish extraordinary things.

**Key Truth**: "But in your hearts revere Christ as Lord. Always be prepared to give an answer to everyone who asks you to give the reason for the hope that you have. But do this with gentleness and respect." - 1 Peter 3:15

## Overcoming Fear and Obstacles

### Common Fears

**1. "I don't know enough."**
- **Truth**: You don't need all the answers. You just need to share what Jesus has done for you.
- **Remember**: The disciples were "unschooled, ordinary men" (Acts 4:13), yet they turned the world upside down.
- **Start with**: "I don't know everything, but I know Jesus changed my life."

**2. "I'm afraid of being rejected."**
- **Truth**: Rejection of the gospel isn't rejection of you personally.
- **Remember**: Jesus warned, "If the world hates you, keep in mind that it hated me first" (John 15:18).
- **Perspective**: Some plant, some water, but God makes things grow (1 Corinthians 3:6-7).

**3. "What if they ask a question I can't answer?"**
- **Truth**: It's okay to say "I don't know."
- **Response**: "That's a great question. I don't know the answer, but I'd love to find out and get back to you."
- **Action**: This gives you a reason to continue the conversation later.

**4. "I don't want to be pushy or annoying."**
- **Truth**: There's a difference between sharing good news and being obnoxious.
- **Balance**: Share with love, respect, and sensitivity. Read the room.
- **Guideline**: "Speaking the truth in love" (Ephesians 4:15).

**5. "My life isn't perfect. I'm a hypocrite."**
- **Truth**: None of us are perfect. That's why we need Jesus.
- **Honesty**: "I'm not perfect, but I'm forgiven. Jesus is changing me."
- **Remember**: God uses broken vessels (2 Corinthians 4:7).

### Building Confidence

**Prepare Your Heart**
- Pray for boldness (Ephesians 6:19-20)
- Remember the gospel is "the power of God for salvation" (Romans 1:16)
- You're a witness, not a lawyer. Just share what you've seen and heard (Acts 4:20)

**Start Small**
- Begin with people you know and care about
- Practice with a Christian friend first
- Share your testimony before trying complex apologetics

**Trust the Holy Spirit**
- Jesus promised: "The Holy Spirit will teach you at that time what you should say" (Luke 12:12)
- Rely on God's power, not your eloquence
- Prayer before, during, and after conversations

## The Foundation: Understanding the Gospel

Before sharing the gospel, make sure you understand it clearly yourself.

### The Core Message

**The Gospel in One Sentence**:
God loves you, you're separated from Him by sin, Jesus died and rose again to save you, and you can be forgiven and have eternal life through faith in Him.

### The Essential Elements

**1. God's Love and Design**
- God created you for relationship with Him
- "For God so loved the world..." (John 3:16)
- You were made for a purpose

**2. The Problem: Sin**
- All have sinned and fall short (Romans 3:23)
- Sin separates us from God (Isaiah 59:2)
- The consequence is death (Romans 6:23a)

**3. The Solution: Jesus**
- God's gift is eternal life through Jesus (Romans 6:23b)
- Jesus died in our place (2 Corinthians 5:21)
- He rose from the dead, defeating death (1 Corinthians 15:3-4)

**4. The Response: Faith**
- Salvation is by grace through faith (Ephesians 2:8-9)
- Confess and believe (Romans 10:9-10)
- Repent and receive new life (Acts 3:19)

### What the Gospel Is NOT

- A self-improvement plan
- "Just be a good person"
- Multiple paths to God
- Earning your way to heaven
- A political or social platform

## Gospel Presentation Methods

Here are proven methods for sharing the gospel clearly and simply.

### Method 1: The Romans Road

A series of verses from Romans that explain the gospel step-by-step.

**Step 1: Everyone Has Sinned**
*"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."* - Romans 3:23

**Step 2: The Penalty for Sin**
*"For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord."* - Romans 6:23

**Step 3: God's Love Demonstrated**
*"But God demonstrates his own love for us in this: While we were still sinners, Christ died for us."* - Romans 5:8

**Step 4: How to Be Saved**
*"If you declare with your mouth, 'Jesus is Lord,' and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved."* - Romans 10:9

**Step 5: The Result**
*"Therefore, since we have been justified through faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ."* - Romans 5:1

### Method 2: The Bridge Illustration

A visual way to explain the gospel using a simple drawing.

```
    US                    |                    GOD
(Sinful)                  |                 (Holy)
                          |
                          |
                     [JESUS CROSS]
                          |
```

**Explain**:
1. Draw two cliffs with a gap between them
2. On one side write "Us" - on the other "God"
3. The gap represents sin that separates us
4. Draw a cross as a bridge connecting the two sides
5. Jesus' death bridges the gap
6. We cross by faith

### Method 3: The Three Circles

A conversational method using three overlapping circles.

**Circle 1: God's Design**
- God created the world perfect
- We were made for relationship with God
- Everything had purpose and meaning

**Circle 2: Brokenness (Sin)**
- We rejected God's design
- Sin entered the world
- Everything is broken: relationships, creation, ourselves
- We try to fix it ourselves (religion, morality, success) but can't

**Circle 3: The Gospel (Good News)**
- Jesus came to restore what was broken
- He lived perfectly, died for our sins, rose again
- He invites us to turn from sin (repent) and believe in Him
- We enter God's design and become agents of restoration

**The Journey**: Move from brokenness → gospel → God's design (new life)

### Method 4: Your Personal Testimony

One of the most powerful tools is your own story. Structure it in three parts:

**Before Jesus** (30 seconds)
- What was your life like before Christ?
- What were you searching for?
- What was missing?

**How You Met Jesus** (30 seconds)
- How did you hear the gospel?
- What convinced you?
- How did you respond?

**After Jesus** (30 seconds)
- How has your life changed?
- What difference does Jesus make?
- What does He mean to you now?

**Example**:
"Before I knew Jesus, I was always searching for purpose. I had success but felt empty. Then a friend invited me to church, and I heard that Jesus loved me enough to die for me. When I understood that I couldn't earn God's love—it was a gift—I surrendered my life to Him. Now I have peace, purpose, and hope that doesn't depend on my circumstances."

### Method 5: The Two Questions Method

**Question 1**: "If you died today, do you know for certain you would go to heaven?"

If they say no or are uncertain:

**Question 2**: "If you stood before God and He asked, 'Why should I let you into heaven?' what would you say?"

Common answers:
- "I'm a good person"
- "I've done more good than bad"
- "I go to church"

**Your Response**:
"Those are common answers, but the Bible says we can't earn our way to heaven. Can I share what the Bible says about how we can know for certain?"

Then share the gospel using one of the methods above.

## Starting Spiritual Conversations

### Natural Conversation Starters

**1. Ask About Their Story**
- "What's your spiritual background?"
- "Did you grow up in a religious home?"
- "Do you have any faith beliefs?"

**2. Share Your Story**
- "Can I tell you about something that changed my life?"
- "I had an interesting experience at church this week..."
- "I was reading something fascinating in the Bible..."

**3. Current Events**
- "What do you think happens after we die?"
- "With everything happening in the world, where do you find hope?"
- "Do you think there's a God?"

**4. Use Your Life Circumstances**
- When facing trials: "My faith has really helped me through this..."
- After a blessing: "I'm so grateful to God for..."
- When they're struggling: "Can I pray for you?"

**5. Ask for Their Opinion**
- "What do you think about Jesus?"
- "Have you ever read the Bible?"
- "What do you think the meaning of life is?"

### The BLESS Method

A framework for building relationships that lead to gospel conversations:

**B - Begin with Prayer**
- Pray for 5 people by name regularly
- Ask God for opportunities

**L - Listen**
- Ask questions about their life
- Show genuine interest
- Care about their struggles

**E - Eat Together**
- Share meals regularly
- Hospitality opens doors
- Jesus ate with sinners

**S - Serve**
- Meet practical needs
- Show love through actions
- Earn the right to be heard

**S - Story**
- Share your testimony
- Tell what Jesus is doing in your life
- Point to Jesus naturally

### Creating Opportunities

**Invite People**
- To church services or events
- To a Bible study or small group
- To Christian concerts or conferences
- To serve with you in ministry

**Be Available**
- Don't be always too busy for conversations
- Linger after work, at the gym, in the neighborhood
- Make time for "interruptions"

**Be Observant**
- Notice when people are hurting or searching
- Ask follow-up questions when they mention struggles
- Look for "divine appointments"

## Listening and Asking Questions

### The Power of Listening

**Why Listening Matters**
- Shows you genuinely care
- Helps you understand their real questions
- Builds trust and credibility
- Jesus asked questions constantly

**How to Listen Well**
- Give full attention (put phone away)
- Don't interrupt or think about your response
- Ask clarifying questions
- Summarize what you heard: "So you're saying..."

### Good Questions to Ask

**Understanding Their Beliefs**
- "What do you believe about God?"
- "How would you describe your spiritual journey?"
- "What do you think about Jesus?"

**Identifying Obstacles**
- "What keeps you from believing in God?"
- "Have you had a bad experience with church or Christians?"
- "What questions do you have about Christianity?"

**Probing Deeper**
- "Why do you believe that?"
- "Where did that idea come from?"
- "Have you always believed that, or did something change?"

**Personal Application**
- "If Christianity were true, how would that change your life?"
- "What would it take for you to believe?"
- "Are you searching for truth, or have you already decided?"

### The Columbo Tactic

Named after the TV detective who asked lots of questions.

**Three Key Questions**:

1. **"What do you mean by that?"**
   - Clarifies their position
   - Helps them think through what they're saying

2. **"How did you come to that conclusion?"**
   - Reveals the foundation of their belief
   - Exposes weak reasoning gently

3. **"Have you considered...?"**
   - Introduces a new perspective
   - Makes them think without being confrontational

**Example**:
- Them: "All religions are basically the same."
- You: "What do you mean by that? In what ways are they the same?"
- Them: "They all teach the same moral values."
- You: "How did you come to that conclusion? Have you studied all the major religions?"
- You: "Have you considered that Christianity uniquely claims salvation is a gift, not something you earn, while most religions teach salvation by works?"

## Responding to Common Questions

### "How can you believe the Bible is true?"

**Brief Response**:
"Great question. The Bible has been incredibly well-preserved through history, and archaeological discoveries consistently support its historical accuracy. But ultimately, I believe it's true because of how it's changed my life and because Jesus validated it by rising from the dead."

**Follow-up**:
Offer to share evidence or recommend resources. See: [Answering Common Objections](answering-objections.md)

### "What about people who never hear about Jesus?"

**Brief Response**:
"That's a question many people ask. The Bible teaches that God is both just and merciful. What I do know is that God judges people based on what they knew and how they responded to it (Romans 2:12-16). The fact that you're asking this question shows you have heard—so the question is, how will you respond?"

**Key Point**: Don't let hypothetical questions distract from their personal decision.

### "Aren't Christians hypocrites?"

**Brief Response**:
"You're absolutely right—many Christians, including me, don't always live up to what we believe. That's actually why we need Jesus. Christianity isn't claiming we're perfect; it's admitting we're broken and need a Savior. The question isn't whether Christians are perfect, but whether Jesus is who He claimed to be."

**Redirect**: "Have you ever looked into who Jesus was and what He claimed?"

### "There are so many religions. How can Christianity be the only way?"

**Brief Response**:
"All religions can't be true because they contradict each other on fundamental issues. Either Jesus rose from the dead or He didn't. Christianity is unique because it's not about what we do to reach God, but what God did to reach us. Jesus claimed to be the only way (John 14:6)—so either He was lying, crazy, or telling the truth."

**Evidence**: Point to the resurrection as the decisive proof. See: [Evidence for the Resurrection](evidence-resurrection.md)

### "What about suffering and evil?"

**Brief Response**:
"If there were no God, we wouldn't even have a standard to call something 'evil.' The fact that we recognize evil points to a moral law, which points to a moral lawgiver. Christianity doesn't ignore suffering—Jesus suffered on the cross. He doesn't promise to remove all suffering now, but He promises to be with us in it and ultimately to end it forever."

**Personal**: "I've experienced suffering too, and my faith has been what sustained me through it."

### "I'm a good person. Isn't that enough?"

**Brief Response**:
"Compared to some people, sure, you might be 'good.' But compared to God's perfect standard, all of us fall short (Romans 3:23). If we could earn our way to heaven by being good, Jesus wouldn't have needed to die. The good news is that salvation is a gift based on what Jesus did, not what we do."

**Illustration**: "If you fell off a boat in the middle of the ocean, being a better swimmer than someone else wouldn't save you. You'd need to be rescued. That's what Jesus does for us."

## Following Up After a Conversation

### If They're Interested

**1. Give Them Something to Read**
- A Gospel of John
- A relevant tract or booklet
- Your contact information

**2. Invite Them to Take a Next Step**
- "Would you be interested in reading the Bible together?"
- "Can I invite you to church with me this Sunday?"
- "Would you like to continue this conversation over coffee?"

**3. Offer to Pray**
- "Can I pray for you right now about what we discussed?"
- "Is there anything specific you'd like me to pray about for you?"

**4. Set a Follow-Up**
- Don't leave it vague: "Let me know if you want to talk again."
- Be specific: "Can we meet next Tuesday to talk more?"
- Text them later: "I really enjoyed our conversation. Still thinking about that question you asked."

### If They're Not Ready

**1. Don't Push**
- Respect their decision
- Leave the door open: "If you ever want to talk about this, I'm here."
- Trust God's timing

**2. Continue the Friendship**
- Don't make the relationship all about evangelism
- Show them Jesus through your life
- Keep praying for them

**3. Plant Seeds**
- Sometimes all you can do is plant a seed
- Others may water it
- God causes the growth (1 Corinthians 3:6-7)

**4. Stay in Touch**
- Send an encouraging text
- Share a relevant article or video
- Continue inviting them to events

## Praying for the Lost

### Why Prayer is Essential

- Only God can open blind eyes (2 Corinthians 4:4)
- Satan blinds the minds of unbelievers (2 Corinthians 4:4)
- We're in a spiritual battle (Ephesians 6:12)
- Prayer invites God's power into the situation

### What to Pray For

**1. For Opportunities**
*"Devote yourselves to prayer, being watchful and thankful. And pray for us, too, that God may open a door for our message."* - Colossians 4:2-3

- Ask God to create divine appointments
- Pray for natural conversation starters
- Ask for wisdom to recognize opportunities

**2. For Boldness**
*"Pray also for me, that whenever I speak, words may be given me so that I will fearlessly make known the mystery of the gospel."* - Ephesians 6:19

- Ask for courage to speak
- Pray against fear and timidity
- Ask for clarity and wisdom

**3. For Their Hearts**
- That God would draw them to Himself (John 6:44)
- That the Holy Spirit would convict them of sin (John 16:8)
- That spiritual blindness would be removed (2 Corinthians 4:4)
- That they would have soft, receptive hearts (Acts 16:14)

**4. For Laborers**
*"The harvest is plentiful but the workers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest, therefore, to send out workers into his harvest field."* - Matthew 9:37-38

- Pray for others who can influence them
- Ask God to send multiple witnesses
- Pray for the global harvest

### Practical Prayer Strategies

**Keep a List**
- Write down names of 5-10 people you're praying for
- Note specific requests for each person
- Record answers to prayer

**Pray Scriptures**
- Pray specific verses over them
- "Lord, open [name]'s eyes to see the light of the gospel" (2 Cor 4:4)
- "Draw [name] to yourself" (John 6:44)

**Fast and Pray**
- Some spiritual strongholds require fasting (Mark 9:29)
- Skip a meal and spend that time praying
- Ask God for breakthrough

## When Someone Says Yes to Jesus

### Leading Them in Prayer

If someone is ready to accept Christ, you can lead them in a prayer. Remember: it's not the exact words that save, but the heart behind them.

**Sample Prayer**:
"Repeat after me if this expresses what's in your heart:

*Dear God,*
*I know I am a sinner and I need Your forgiveness.*
*I believe that Jesus Christ died for my sins and rose from the dead.*
*I want to turn from my sins and invite Jesus to come into my heart and life.*
*I want to trust and follow Him as my Lord and Savior.*
*In Jesus' name, Amen.*

Did you pray that and mean it from your heart?"

### Immediate Next Steps

**1. Assure Them of Salvation**
- Read them God's promise: "Everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved" (Romans 10:13)
- Explain that salvation is secure based on God's promise, not feelings
- Remind them: "If you declare with your mouth, 'Jesus is Lord,' and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved" (Romans 10:9)

**2. Explain What Just Happened**
- They've been forgiven of all sins (1 John 1:9)
- They've received eternal life (John 3:16)
- They've become a child of God (John 1:12)
- The Holy Spirit now lives in them (1 Corinthians 3:16)
- They're a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17)

**3. Give Them Resources**
- A Bible (especially if they don't have one)
- New Believer's materials or booklet
- See: [New Believer's Guide](new-believer-guide.md)

**4. Connect Them to Community**
- Invite them to church with you
- Introduce them to other believers
- Don't leave them isolated

**5. Plan to Follow Up**
- Get their contact information
- Schedule a specific time to meet again
- Text them the next day to encourage them

### Disciple Them

Your job isn't done when they pray the prayer. Now the real work begins.

**The Great Commission** doesn't just say "make converts"—it says "make disciples" (Matthew 28:19-20).

**What New Believers Need**:
- Regular encouragement and accountability
- Help understanding the Bible
- Answers to their questions
- Connection to a church community
- Examples of Christian living

**Simple Discipleship Plan**:
1. Meet regularly (weekly if possible)
2. Read the Bible together (start with Gospel of John)
3. Pray together
4. Answer their questions
5. Help them take next steps (baptism, church membership, serving)

## Practical Examples and Scripts

### Example 1: Coffee Shop Conversation

**You**: "Hey, I've been thinking about that conversation we had last week about purpose. Can I share something that's really helped me?"

**Them**: "Sure, what's that?"

**You**: "I used to think I had to create my own purpose, and honestly, it was exhausting. But then I discovered that God created me with a purpose—not something I had to earn or achieve, but something already designed for me. That's when everything clicked."

**Them**: "That's interesting. How did you figure out what that purpose was?"

**You**: "It started when I understood the gospel—the good news about Jesus. Can I share it with you real quick? It'll take like 2 minutes."

*(Then use Romans Road or Bridge Illustration)*

### Example 2: Responding to Suffering

**Them**: "I don't know how you can believe in God with everything going on in the world."

**You**: "I totally understand that question. I've wrestled with it too, especially when I [share brief personal experience with suffering]. Can I tell you what helped me?"

**Them**: "Yeah, I'd like to hear."

**You**: "I realized that if there's no God, then suffering has no meaning at all—it's just random chaos. But Christianity teaches that God didn't stay distant from suffering. He entered into it. Jesus suffered on the cross so that one day He could end all suffering forever. That gives me hope that my pain isn't meaningless."

**Them**: "I never thought about it that way."

**You**: "Yeah, it changed my perspective. Have you ever read about Jesus' death and resurrection?"

*(Transition to the gospel)*

### Example 3: Using Your Testimony

**Them**: "You seem really peaceful. How do you handle stress?"

**You**: "Honestly, I haven't always been this way. Before I knew Jesus, I was anxious all the time. I tried to control everything, and when things didn't go my way, I'd fall apart."

**Them**: "So what changed?"

**You**: "I heard about Jesus at a really low point in my life. Someone explained to me that I didn't have to carry all my burdens alone—that Jesus already carried them on the cross. When I surrendered my life to Him, I found a peace I'd never experienced before. Don't get me wrong, I still face stress, but now I have somewhere to take it."

**Them**: "That sounds nice. How did you 'surrender your life'?"

*(Share the gospel and explain how they can do the same)*

### Example 4: The Direct Approach

**You**: "Can I ask you a spiritual question?"

**Them**: "Uh, sure?"

**You**: "If you died tonight, do you know for certain you'd go to heaven?"

**Them**: "I don't know. I hope so?"

**You**: "What would you say if God asked you, 'Why should I let you into heaven?'"

**Them**: "I guess I'd say I'm a pretty good person. I try to do the right thing."

**You**: "That's what most people say. But can I show you what the Bible says about how we can know for certain? It's actually really good news."

*(Use Romans Road or another method)*

### Example 5: Responding to Relativism

**Them**: "That's great that Christianity works for you, but I think everyone has to find their own truth."

**You**: "I appreciate that you respect my beliefs. Can I ask you a question? If Christianity is true—like, actually true, not just true for me—would you want to know?"

**Them**: "Well, sure, but how can anyone know what's really true?"

**You**: "Great question. That's exactly what I asked before I became a Christian. What convinced me was the historical evidence for Jesus rising from the dead. If that actually happened, it changes everything, right? It would mean Jesus really was who He said He was."

**Them**: "I guess. But did that really happen?"

**You**: "Yes, and there's really strong evidence for it. Can I share some of it with you?"

*(Share evidence or refer them to resources. See: [Evidence for the Resurrection](evidence-resurrection.md))*

## Final Encouragement

### Remember These Truths

**1. It's God's Work, Not Yours**
- You're a witness, not the Savior
- You plant and water; God causes growth (1 Corinthians 3:6-7)
- The Holy Spirit convicts, not you (John 16:8)

**2. You Don't Need to Be Perfect**
- God uses broken vessels (2 Corinthians 4:7)
- Your weakness highlights His strength
- Authenticity is more powerful than perfection

**3. Faithfulness, Not Success**
- You're called to be faithful, not necessarily successful
- Some plant, some water, some harvest
- You may never see the fruit of your labor

**4. Every Conversation Matters**
- You don't know what God is already doing in their heart
- One conversation can plant a seed that grows for years
- Be faithful in the small opportunities

### Practical Reminders

**Start Today**
- Don't wait until you feel "ready"
- Begin with people you already know
- Take small steps of obedience

**Make It a Lifestyle**
- Evangelism isn't an event; it's a way of life
- Be ready in season and out of season (2 Timothy 4:2)
- Look for opportunities everywhere

**Stay Connected to Jesus**
- You can't give what you don't have
- Abide in Christ (John 15:4-5)
- Keep your own relationship with God vibrant

**Don't Go Alone**
- Partner with other believers
- Invite someone to go with you
- Share stories and encourage each other

### You Can Do This

God has placed you exactly where you are for a reason. The people in your life—your family, coworkers, neighbors, friends—they're not there by accident. You may be the only Christian they know. You may be the only Bible they ever read.

The gospel is "the power of God for salvation" (Romans 1:16). It's not your power, your eloquence, or your intelligence that saves people. It's the gospel itself. Your job is simply to share it.

So go. Speak up. Share Jesus. You have the greatest news the world has ever heard.

**"How beautiful are the feet of those who bring good news!"** - Romans 10:15
