# The Intertestamental Period

Understanding the 400 "Silent Years" between the Testaments

---

## Introduction

**"Then Herod called the Magi secretly and found out from them the exact time the star had appeared." - Matthew 2:7**

When you finish reading Malachi (last book of the Old Testament) and turn the page to Matthew (first book of the New Testament), **400 years have passed**.

During those 400 years:
- No prophets spoke
- No Scripture was written
- But God was NOT silent
- The world was being prepared for Jesus

**This guide explains what happened during those crucial centuries and why it matters for understanding the New Testament.**

## What Is the Intertestamental Period?

### The Time Gap

**Old Testament ends:** ~400 BC (Malachi)
**New Testament begins:** ~5 BC (Jesus' birth)
**Time elapsed:** ~400 years

**Alternative names:**
- "The Silent Years" (no prophets)
- "The 400 Years of Silence"
- "The Second Temple Period" (Temple rebuilt)
- "Between the Testaments"

### God Was Not Absent

**"Silent" means:**
- No prophets raised up
- No Scripture inspired
- No direct revelation

**But God WAS working:**
- Preserving His people
- Arranging world events
- Preparing for the Messiah
- Fulfilling prophecy

**"When the set time had fully come, God sent his Son." - Galatians 4:4**

---

## Why Does It Matter?

### Reason 1: The New Testament World Is Different

**When Malachi ends:**
- Jews returned from Babylon
- Persian Empire rules
- Hebrew is spoken
- Temple rebuilt
- No Pharisees, Sadducees, or synagogues

**When Matthew begins:**
- Roman Empire rules
- Greek is spoken everywhere
- Pharisees and Sadducees dominate
- Synagogues in every town
- Herod (an Idumean) is "King of the Jews"

**Question:** How did all this change happen?
**Answer:** The Intertestamental Period

---

### Reason 2: Jesus' Context Makes Sense

**Without knowing this period, you'll wonder:**
- Who are the Pharisees and Sadducees?
- Why do Jews hate Samaritans so much?
- Why is Herod called "king" if he's not Jewish?
- Why does everyone speak Greek?
- What's a synagogue? (Not mentioned in OT)
- Why do Jews expect a warrior Messiah?

**The Intertestamental Period answers all these questions.**

---

### Reason 3: God's Sovereignty Is Evident

**God orchestrated:**
- Greek language spread (for New Testament)
- Roman roads built (for spreading gospel)
- Jewish diaspora (synagogues everywhere for Paul to preach)
- Messianic expectation heightened (people watching for deliverer)

**"In the fullness of time" wasn't random. God was preparing the world for Jesus.**

## Timeline at a Glance

### 538-332 BC: Persian Period (~200 years)
- Jews return from Babylonian exile
- Temple rebuilt (completed 516 BC)
- Ezra and Nehemiah's reforms
- Relative peace and autonomy

### 332-167 BC: Greek Period (~165 years)
- Alexander the Great conquers Persia (332 BC)
- Hellenization (Greek culture) spreads
- Jews divided: embrace or resist Greek culture?
- Ptolemies rule (Egypt-based, 323-198 BC)
- Seleucids rule (Syria-based, 198-167 BC)

### 167-63 BC: Maccabean/Hasmonean Period (~100 years)
- Antiochus IV Epiphanes defiles Temple (167 BC)
- Maccabean Revolt (167-160 BC)
- Jewish independence achieved
- Hasmonean dynasty rules
- Decline into corruption

### 63 BC-AD 70: Roman Period
- Rome conquers Jerusalem (63 BC)
- Herod the Great rules (37-4 BC)
- Jesus born (~5-4 BC)
- New Testament era begins

## The Four Empires

### 1. Persian Period (538-332 BC)

**Key Events:**
- **538 BC:** Cyrus of Persia allows Jews to return
- **516 BC:** Second Temple completed
- **458 BC:** Ezra returns with more exiles
- **445 BC:** Nehemiah rebuilds Jerusalem's walls
- **~430 BC:** Malachi (last prophet)

**Characteristics:**
- Persian rule was tolerant
- Jews had religious freedom
- Small community in Judea
- Many Jews remained in Persia (diaspora begins)

**Why It Ended:**
Alexander the Great defeated Persia in 332 BC

---

### 2. Greek Period (332-167 BC)

#### A. Alexander the Great (332-323 BC)

**Who:** Macedonian military genius conquered known world by age 30

**Impact on Jews:**
- Conquered Judea in 332 BC
- Generally tolerant of Jews
- Spread Greek language and culture (Hellenization)
- Died in 323 BC at age 32

**Prophecy Fulfilled:**
Daniel 8:5-8 predicted Greece would conquer Persia

---

#### B. The Divided Kingdom (323-167 BC)

**After Alexander's death, his empire divided among four generals (Daniel 8:8, 22):**

**Two matter for Jewish history:**

**1. Ptolemies (Egypt) - Ruled Judea 323-198 BC**
- Relatively peaceful period
- Jews allowed to practice religion
- Alexandria, Egypt becomes major Jewish center
- **Septuagint** (Greek OT) translated (~250 BC)

**2. Seleucids (Syria) - Ruled Judea 198-167 BC**
- Much harsher than Ptolemies
- Aggressive Hellenization
- Tried to force Greek culture on Jews
- Led to crisis under Antiochus IV

---

#### C. Antiochus IV Epiphanes (175-164 BC)

**Who:** Seleucid king, determined to Hellenize all subjects

**His Actions Against Jews:**
- Outlawed Jewish practices (circumcision, Sabbath, Torah)
- Desecrated Temple (167 BC):
  - Sacrificed pig on altar
  - Erected statue of Zeus in Temple
  - Called it "the abomination of desolation" (Daniel 11:31; cf. Matthew 24:15)
- Killed Jews who resisted

**Result:** This sparked the Maccabean Revolt

**Prophecy Fulfilled:**
- Daniel 8:9-14 (little horn)
- Daniel 11:21-35 (contemptible person)

---

### 3. Maccabean/Hasmonean Period (167-63 BC)

#### A. The Maccabean Revolt (167-160 BC)

**Who:** Judas Maccabeus ("the Hammer") and his family

**What Happened:**
- **167 BC:** Priest Mattathias refused to sacrifice to Greek gods; killed Greek official; fled to hills with sons
- **166 BC:** Mattathias died; son Judas took leadership
- **165 BC:** Guerrilla warfare against Seleucids
- **164 BC:** Recaptured Jerusalem; cleansed and rededicated Temple
  - **This is celebrated as Hanukkah** (John 10:22 - "Feast of Dedication")
- **160 BC:** Judas killed in battle

**Significance:**
- Miraculous victory (small band vs. empire)
- Preserved Jewish faith and identity
- Achieved independence for first time since Babylonian exile

---

#### B. The Hasmonean Dynasty (142-63 BC)

**Who:** Descendants of the Maccabees

**Early Years (142-110 BC):**
- **142 BC:** Simon (Judas' brother) declared independence
- Jewish self-rule achieved
- Peace and prosperity
- Expansion of territory

**Later Years (110-63 BC):**
- Corruption set in
- Priest-kings (combining roles God had separated)
- Internal conflict
- Decline into civil war

**Key Figures:**
- **John Hyrcanus** (134-104 BC) - Forced Idumeans (Edomites) to convert to Judaism
  - This matters because Herod the Great was Idumean
- **Alexander Jannaeus** (103-76 BC) - Brutal ruler, civil war
- **Hyrcanus II vs. Aristobulus II** (67-63 BC) - Brothers fighting for throne

**How It Ended:**
Both brothers appealed to Rome for help. Rome conquered instead.

---

### 4. Roman Period (63 BC - AD 70+)

#### A. Roman Conquest (63 BC)

**Who:** Pompey, Roman general

**What:** Conquered Jerusalem in 63 BC
- Judea became Roman territory
- Ended Jewish independence
- Would not regain independence until 1948 AD

---

#### B. Herod the Great (37-4 BC)

**Who:** Idumean (Edomite), not ethnically Jewish
- Rome appointed him "King of the Jews"
- Brutal, paranoid, brilliant builder

**Why He Matters:**
- **Matthew 2** - Tried to kill baby Jesus
- Renovated Temple (Herod's Temple)
- Established dynasty (Herod Antipas, Herod Agrippa in NT)

**His Reign:**
- **37 BC:** Became king
- **20 BC:** Began Temple renovation
- **~5-4 BC:** Jesus born
- **4 BC:** Herod died (Matthew 2:19-20)

**Character:**
- Killed his own wife and sons (paranoia)
- Augustus Caesar said it's safer to be Herod's pig than his son
  - (Jews don't eat pork, so pig would be safe!)

## Religious Developments

### New Groups Emerge

**In Malachi's time (400 BC):** Priests and prophets
**In Jesus' time (AD 30):** Pharisees, Sadducees, Essenes, Zealots, scribes

**Where did they come from?** The Intertestamental Period.

---

### 1. Pharisees

**Origin:** During Greek/Maccabean period

**Name:** "Separated ones"

**Beliefs:**
- Strict obedience to Torah AND oral traditions
- Believed in resurrection, angels, afterlife
- Emphasized purity laws
- Awaited Messiah

**Goals:**
- Preserve Jewish identity against Greek culture
- Apply Torah to every area of life

**In the NT:**
- Often opposed Jesus (Matthew 23)
- But some became believers (Acts 15:5; Paul was a Pharisee - Philippians 3:5)
- Most influential group after AD 70

**Modern Connection:**
Rabbinic Judaism descended from Pharisees

---

### 2. Sadducees

**Origin:** Maccabean period, from priestly aristocracy

**Name:** Possibly from Zadok (priestly line)

**Beliefs:**
- Only Torah (first 5 books) is authoritative
- No resurrection, angels, or afterlife (Acts 23:8)
- Collaborated with Romans

**Goals:**
- Maintain Temple control
- Political power and wealth
- Status quo

**In the NT:**
- Opposed Jesus (tried Him)
- Opposed apostles (Acts 4-5)
- Rejected resurrection doctrine

**What Happened:**
Disappeared after Temple destroyed in AD 70 (no Temple = no power)

---

### 3. Essenes

**Origin:** ~150 BC, broke away during Maccabean conflicts

**Who:** Separatist sect, lived in desert (Qumran)

**Beliefs:**
- Awaited two Messiahs (priestly and kingly)
- Emphasized purity
- Practiced communal living
- Copied Scripture meticulously

**Not Mentioned in NT:**
Probably because they isolated themselves

**Significance:**
- Dead Sea Scrolls (discovered 1947) came from Essenes
- Scrolls prove OT text preserved accurately

---

### 4. Zealots

**Origin:** 1st century AD

**Who:** Revolutionary militants

**Goal:** Overthrow Rome by force

**Beliefs:**
- No king but God
- Awaited warrior Messiah
- Violence justified against oppressors

**In the NT:**
- Simon the Zealot (disciple - Luke 6:15)
- Barabbas likely a Zealot (Mark 15:7)
- Crowd wanted Jesus to be this kind of Messiah (John 6:15)

**What Happened:**
Led revolt in AD 66-70; Rome destroyed Jerusalem and Temple

---

### 5. Scribes

**Origin:** During exile and after

**Who:** Experts in the Law (Torah)

**Role:**
- Copied Scripture
- Taught the Law
- Interpreted difficult passages
- Legal experts

**In the NT:**
- Often grouped with Pharisees (Matthew 23:2)
- Some opposed Jesus, some believed (Mark 12:28-34)

---

### 6. Samaritans

**Background:**
- **722 BC:** Assyria conquered Northern Kingdom (Israel)
- Foreigners settled in Samaria
- Intermarried with remaining Jews
- Mixed worship of Yahweh with pagan practices

**By Intertestamental Period:**
- Samaritans built rival temple on Mt. Gerizim (~400 BC)
- **128 BC:** John Hyrcanus destroyed their temple
- Deep hatred between Jews and Samaritans

**In the NT:**
- Jews avoided Samaria (John 4:9)
- Jesus' parable of Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25-37)
- Jesus spoke to Samaritan woman (John 4)
- Gospel spread to Samaria (Acts 8)

---

### The Synagogue

**Origin:** During Babylonian exile, developed fully in Intertestamental Period

**Purpose:**
- When Temple far away, need local place to worship
- Study Torah, pray, community gathering

**Structure:**
- Any town with 10 Jewish men could have synagogue
- Ruler of synagogue
- Scriptures read and explained (Luke 4:16-21)

**In the NT:**
- Jesus taught in synagogues (Matthew 4:23)
- Paul preached in synagogues (Acts 13:14-15)
- Everywhere in Roman world

**Significance:**
Prepared infrastructure for spreading gospel

---

### The Sanhedrin

**What:** Jewish ruling council, 71 members

**Origin:** Persian period, formalized later

**Composition:**
- High priest (president)
- Sadducees (priestly aristocracy)
- Pharisees (legal experts)
- Elders

**Authority:**
- Religious matters
- Some civil authority
- Could not execute (under Rome) - John 18:31

**In the NT:**
- Tried Jesus (Matthew 26:57-68)
- Tried apostles (Acts 4-5)
- Paul brought before (Acts 22:30-23:10)

## The Apocrypha

### What Is It?

**Apocrypha:** Books written during Intertestamental Period, included in some Bibles

**Books Include:**
- 1-2 Maccabees (history of Maccabean revolt)
- Tobit
- Judith
- Wisdom of Solomon
- Sirach (Ecclesiasticus)
- Baruch
- Additions to Esther and Daniel
- And others

---

### Why Are They Debated?

**Not in Hebrew Bible:**
- Jews never accepted as Scripture
- Not in Jesus' Bible

**In Septuagint (Greek OT):**
- Greek-speaking Jews used this translation
- Included these books

**Early Church Views:**
- Some church fathers quoted them
- Others rejected them as non-canonical
- Debates continued

**Protestant Reformation:**
- Luther and Reformers: Useful for history but not Scripture
- Removed from Protestant Bibles
- "Sola Scriptura" - only 66 books inspired

**Catholic/Orthodox:**
- Consider them "Deuterocanonical" (second canon)
- Include in Bibles

---

### Are They Scripture?

**Reasons Protestants Reject:**
1. Not in Hebrew Bible
2. Jesus never quoted them (quoted all OT sections)
3. No "Thus says the LORD"
4. Historical/theological errors
5. Written during "silent" period (no prophets)

**Still Valuable For:**
- Historical information (Maccabean period)
- Understanding Jewish thought
- Context for NT

**Example:**
1 Maccabees explains Hanukkah, which Jesus attended (John 10:22)

## How the World Changed

### Political Changes

| Malachi (400 BC) | Matthew (5 BC) |
|------------------|----------------|
| Persian Empire | Roman Empire |
| Jews have autonomy | Jews under Roman occupation |
| Persian governors | King Herod (Idumean) |
| Relative peace | Heavy taxation, oppression |
| No Messiah expectation | Intense Messiah expectation |

---

### Cultural Changes

| Malachi (400 BC) | Matthew (5 BC) |
|------------------|----------------|
| Hebrew spoken | Aramaic spoken, Greek widespread |
| Jewish culture dominant | Greek culture everywhere (Hellenism) |
| Persia is world power | Rome is world power |
| Few Jews outside Judea | Jews scattered across empire |

---

### Religious Changes

| Malachi (400 BC) | Matthew (5 BC) |
|------------------|----------------|
| Temple and prophets | Temple, but no prophets |
| Priests lead | Pharisees, Sadducees, scribes |
| No synagogues mentioned | Synagogues everywhere |
| Simple Judaism | Complex oral traditions |
| Unified Judaism | Divided sects |

---

### Infrastructure for the Gospel

**God prepared the world:**

1. **Greek Language**
   - Alexander spread Greek everywhere
   - Common language for NT and early church
   - Gospel could spread rapidly

2. **Roman Roads and Peace**
   - "Pax Romana" (Roman Peace)
   - Safe travel across empire
   - Roads connected all major cities
   - Perfect for missionaries

3. **Jewish Diaspora**
   - Jews in every major city
   - Synagogues everywhere
   - Starting point for Paul's ministry (Acts 13:14, etc.)
   - "God-fearers" (Gentiles attracted to Judaism) ready to hear gospel

4. **Messianic Expectation**
   - Centuries of oppression
   - Longing for deliverer
   - "Is he the one?" (Matthew 11:3)
   - Simeon and Anna watching (Luke 2:25-38)

**"When the set time had fully come, God sent his Son." - Galatians 4:4**

## Setting the Stage for Jesus

### The World Jesus Entered

**Politically:**
- Roman occupation
- Heavy taxation
- Herod family (brutal, not legitimate)
- Revolutionary movements brewing

**Religiously:**
- Complex system of laws and traditions
- Pharisees (legalism)
- Sadducees (compromise with Rome)
- Temple controlled by corrupt priests
- Messiah expected but misunderstood

**Socially:**
- Hellenism vs. Judaism tension
- Rich vs. poor divide
- Pure Jews vs. "unclean" (tax collectors, sinners, Samaritans)
- Women and children marginalized
- Sick and disabled outcasts

**Expectations:**
- Warrior Messiah to defeat Rome
- Restore Kingdom of Israel
- Political deliverance
- Earthly reign

**What They Got:**
- Suffering Servant
- Spiritual deliverance
- "My kingdom is not of this world" (John 18:36)
- Cross before crown

---

### Why Jesus Was Rejected

**They Expected:**
King like David → overthrow Rome → rule from Jerusalem

**Jesus Came:**
Servant → die for sins → spiritual kingdom

**Their Categories:**
- Prophet
- Priest
- King
- Revolutionary

**Jesus Was:**
All of these, but not how they expected

**The Intertestamental Period shaped these expectations.**

## Prophecies Fulfilled in the "Silent Years"

### Daniel's Prophecies

**Daniel 2:** Four kingdoms (Babylon, Persia, Greece, Rome)
- Fulfilled during Intertestamental Period and beyond

**Daniel 7:** Same four kingdoms as beasts
- Fulfilled as predicted

**Daniel 8:** Ram (Persia) and goat (Greece)
- Alexander explicitly predicted
- Fulfilled in 332 BC

**Daniel 9:24-27:** 70 weeks prophecy
- Timeline leading to Messiah
- "From the time the word goes out to restore Jerusalem until Messiah the Prince..."
- Many see fulfillment in Jesus' first coming

**Daniel 11:** Detailed prophecy of Ptolemies, Seleucids, Antiochus IV
- So accurate some critics claim it was written after the fact
- Actually written ~530 BC, fulfilled ~200-160 BC

---

### Why Daniel Matters

**Written ~530 BC**
**Predicted in detail:**
- Persian rule
- Greek conquest
- Division of Alexander's kingdom
- Conflicts between Ptolemies and Seleucids
- Antiochus IV's persecution
- Maccabean deliverance
- Coming of Messiah

**All fulfilled during Intertestamental Period and NT times**

**Proves:**
- God knows the future
- God is sovereign over history
- Scripture is reliable
- Jesus came exactly when prophesied

## Practical Applications

### For Bible Reading

**When you read the NT, you'll now understand:**
- Why Pharisees and Sadducees exist
- Why Jews hate Romans and tax collectors
- Why Herod had no legitimate claim to throne
- Why everyone expects a political Messiah
- What Hanukkah is (John 10:22)
- Why synagogues are everywhere
- Why Greek is spoken
- Why Samaritans are so hated

---

### For Apologetics

**The Intertestamental Period proves:**

1. **Biblical Prophecy Is Accurate**
   - Daniel predicted these events centuries before
   - Fulfilled exactly as written

2. **God's Sovereignty Over History**
   - Even during "silence," God was working
   - Orchestrating world events for Jesus' coming

3. **Jesus Came at the Perfect Time**
   - Infrastructure in place
   - Language unified
   - Messianic expectation heightened
   - "Fullness of time" (Galatians 4:4)

---

### For Appreciating God's Plan

**400 years without a prophet, but:**
- God preserved His people through persecution
- God protected His Word (Dead Sea Scrolls prove this)
- God prepared the world for the gospel
- God fulfilled prophecy to the day

**When it seems like God is silent, He's still working.**

---

## Conclusion

**The Intertestamental Period was NOT silent in the sense of God's absence. It was the calm before the storm, the preparation before the performance, the setup for the greatest event in history: God becoming man.**

**"When the set time had fully come, God sent his Son, born of a woman, born under the law, to redeem those under the law, that we might receive adoption to sonship." - Galatians 4:4-5**

Everything was ready:
- Language prepared (Greek)
- Roads built (Roman)
- Messianic expectation heightened
- World stage set

**And then:** "In the beginning was the Word... and the Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us" (John 1:1, 14).
