# Biblical Covenants

A comprehensive guide to understanding God's covenants throughout Scripture - the backbone of God's redemptive plan and how He relates to His people.

---

## What is a Covenant?

**Definition:** A covenant is a solemn, binding agreement between two parties with specific promises, obligations, and often ceremonial elements.

**In the Bible:** Covenants are God's way of establishing and maintaining relationships with His people. They reveal His character, promises, and plan for redemption.

**Two Types:**
1. **Conditional:** "If you obey, then I will bless" (Mosaic Covenant)
2. **Unconditional:** "I will..." regardless of human response (Abrahamic, Davidic, New Covenant)

**Why Covenants Matter:**
- They structure the entire biblical narrative
- They reveal God's faithfulness across generations
- They show the progression of God's redemptive plan
- They all ultimately point to Jesus Christ

## Covenant with Creation (Edenic/Adamic)

### The Covenant of Works (Eden)

**Participants:** God and Adam (representing humanity)

**Scripture:** Genesis 1:26-30; 2:15-17

**Time Period:** Before the Fall

**Key Elements:**
- **Provision:** Garden of Eden, food, companionship
- **Commission:** "Be fruitful and multiply; fill the earth and subdue it" (Genesis 1:28)
- **Prohibition:** Don't eat from the tree of knowledge of good and evil
- **Consequence:** Death if disobeyed

**Type:** Conditional - obedience required

**Status:** Broken by Adam and Eve's sin

**Sign:** Tree of Life (access to eternal life)

**Significance:**
- Establishes God's sovereign authority
- Shows humanity's original purpose: fellowship with God, stewardship of creation
- Foundation for understanding sin and need for redemption
- Sets up the need for a new covenant

**Key Verse:**
- Genesis 2:16-17 - "You are free to eat from any tree in the garden; but you must not eat from the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, for when you eat from it you will certainly die"

**Application:** Humanity failed to keep this covenant, showing we need a Savior who perfectly obeys on our behalf.

---

### The Covenant After the Fall

**Scripture:** Genesis 3:14-19

**Key Elements:**
- Consequences of sin announced (pain, toil, death)
- **First gospel promise (Protoevangelium):** Seed of woman will crush serpent's head (Genesis 3:15)
- Expulsion from Eden
- Access to Tree of Life blocked

**Significance:**
- First promise of a Redeemer
- Sets in motion God's plan to restore relationship
- Jesus is the "seed of woman" who crushes Satan

**Key Verse:**
- Genesis 3:15 - "I will put enmity between you and the woman, and between your offspring and hers; he will crush your head, and you will strike his heel"

## Noahic Covenant

**Participants:** God and Noah, his descendants, all creation

**Scripture:** Genesis 6:18; 9:8-17

**Time Period:** After the Flood (~2350 BC)

**Key Elements:**
- God will never again destroy the earth with a flood
- Seasons and natural order preserved
- Animals and plants given to humans for food
- Capital punishment for murder established
- Preservation of creation until final judgment

**Type:** Unconditional - God's promise to all creation

**Sign:** Rainbow - visible reminder of God's promise

**Scope:** Universal - includes all humanity and creation

**Significance:**
- God's common grace to all humanity
- Guarantees stability of creation
- Shows God's mercy despite humanity's wickedness
- Provides framework for human society

**Key Verses:**
- Genesis 9:11 - "I establish my covenant with you: Never again will all life be destroyed by the waters of a flood; never again will there be a flood to destroy the earth"
- Genesis 9:13 - "I have set my rainbow in the clouds, and it will be the sign of the covenant between me and the earth"

**Application:** Every rainbow reminds us of God's faithfulness and patience. He preserves creation while working out His redemptive plan.

## Abrahamic Covenant

**Participants:** God and Abraham (and his descendants)

**Scripture:** Genesis 12:1-3; 15:1-21; 17:1-27; 22:15-18

**Time Period:** ~2000 BC

**Key Elements - God promises Abraham:**
1. **Land:** "To your offspring I will give this land" (Canaan)
2. **Descendants:** "I will make your offspring like the dust of the earth" (innumerable)
3. **Blessing:** "All peoples on earth will be blessed through you"

**Four Stages:**
1. **Initial Call** (Genesis 12:1-3) - Leave your country; I will bless you
2. **Covenant Ceremony** (Genesis 15) - God passes between the pieces; ratifies unconditionally
3. **Covenant of Circumcision** (Genesis 17) - Sign given; Sarai becomes Sarah; promise of Isaac
4. **Confirmed After Test** (Genesis 22) - Abraham willing to sacrifice Isaac; covenant reaffirmed

**Type:** Unconditional - depends on God's faithfulness, not Abraham's performance

**Sign:** Circumcision (Genesis 17:10-14)

**Scope:** Israel and all nations (through the "seed" - Jesus)

**Significance:**
- Foundation for Israel's existence
- God chooses one family to bless all families
- Points to Jesus (the ultimate "seed") through whom all nations blessed
- Land promise still being fulfilled
- Demonstrates salvation by grace through faith

**Key Verses:**
- Genesis 12:2-3 - "I will make you into a great nation, and I will bless you... and all peoples on earth will be blessed through you"
- Genesis 15:6 - "Abram believed the LORD, and he credited it to him as righteousness"
- Genesis 17:7 - "I will establish my covenant as an everlasting covenant between me and you and your descendants after you"

**New Testament Fulfillment:**
- Galatians 3:8 - Gospel preached to Abraham
- Galatians 3:16 - "Seed" is Christ
- Galatians 3:29 - Believers are Abraham's seed
- Romans 4 - Abraham justified by faith, not works

**Application:** God's promises are sure. He chose Abraham by grace, and He chooses us by grace. We are blessed to be a blessing.

## Mosaic Covenant

**Participants:** God and the nation of Israel

**Scripture:** Exodus 19-24; Deuteronomy

**Time Period:** ~1445 BC (at Mount Sinai)

**Key Elements:**
- **The Law:** Moral (Ten Commandments), Civil (for society), Ceremonial (worship/sacrifices)
- **Priesthood:** Levitical priests mediate between God and people
- **Tabernacle/Temple:** God's dwelling place among His people
- **Sacrificial System:** Blood sacrifices for atonement of sin
- **Blessings and Curses:** Obedience brings blessing; disobedience brings curse (Deuteronomy 28)

**Type:** Conditional - "If you obey my voice... you shall be my treasured possession" (Exodus 19:5)

**Sign:** Sabbath observance (Exodus 31:16-17)

**Scope:** National Israel

**Purpose:**
1. **Reveal God's holiness** - His perfect standard
2. **Reveal humanity's sinfulness** - No one can keep the law perfectly
3. **Provide temporary atonement** - Through sacrifices
4. **Set Israel apart** - As God's holy nation
5. **Point forward to Christ** - The law is a schoolmaster/tutor (Galatians 3:24)

**Three Parts of the Law:**
1. **Moral Law:** The Ten Commandments - eternal moral principles
2. **Civil Law:** Laws for governing Israel as a nation - expired with Israel's theocracy
3. **Ceremonial Law:** Sacrifices, priests, feasts - fulfilled in Christ

**Significance:**
- Shows that righteousness can't be achieved by human effort
- Creates context for understanding sin and grace
- Types and shadows point to Christ
- Temporary covenant meant to lead to permanent solution

**Key Verses:**
- Exodus 19:5-6 - "If you obey me fully and keep my covenant, then out of all nations you will be my treasured possession... a kingdom of priests and a holy nation"
- Leviticus 19:2 - "Be holy because I, the LORD your God, am holy"
- Deuteronomy 6:4-5 - "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God, the LORD is one. Love the LORD your God with all your heart"

**New Testament Perspective:**
- Romans 3:20 - "Through the law we become conscious of our sin"
- Galatians 3:24 - "The law was our guardian until Christ came"
- Hebrews 10:1 - "The law is only a shadow of the good things that are coming"
- Romans 10:4 - "Christ is the culmination of the law"

**Status:** Fulfilled in Christ; believers not under Mosaic Law but under New Covenant

**Application:** The law shows you can't save yourself. It drives you to Christ. The moral principles still reflect God's character, but you're not saved by keeping laws - you're saved by grace.

## Davidic Covenant

**Participants:** God and David (and his dynasty)

**Scripture:** 2 Samuel 7:8-16; Psalm 89; Psalm 132

**Time Period:** ~1000 BC

**Key Elements - God promises David:**
1. **Dynasty:** David's descendants will rule forever
2. **Throne:** An eternal throne
3. **Kingdom:** An everlasting kingdom
4. **Son:** A son who will build God's house
5. **Father-Son Relationship:** "I will be his father, and he will be my son"

**Type:** Unconditional - based on God's faithfulness, not David's or his sons'

**Sign:** Davidic lineage continued despite unfaithful kings

**Scope:** Israel, but ultimately all nations under Messiah's rule

**Significance:**
- Narrowing of Messianic promise: Messiah will come from David's line
- Even when kings failed, God preserved the line
- Ultimately fulfilled in Jesus Christ
- Jesus' eternal kingdom has no end

**Key Verses:**
- 2 Samuel 7:12-13 - "I will raise up your offspring to succeed you... He is the one who will build a house for my Name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever"
- 2 Samuel 7:16 - "Your house and your kingdom will endure forever before me; your throne will be established forever"
- Psalm 89:3-4 - "I have made a covenant with my chosen one, I have sworn to David my servant, 'I will establish your line forever and make your throne firm through all generations'"

**Fulfillment in Jesus:**
- Matthew 1:1 - "Jesus the Messiah the son of David"
- Luke 1:32-33 - "The Lord God will give him the throne of his father David, and he will reign over Jacob's descendants forever; his kingdom will never end"
- Acts 2:29-36 - Peter preaches Jesus as David's descendant raised to sit on throne
- Revelation 22:16 - "I am the Root and the Offspring of David"

**Application:** Jesus is the promised eternal King. His kingdom will never end. Submit to His rightful reign as the Son of David.

## New Covenant

**Participants:** God and all believers (Jew and Gentile)

**Scripture:** Jeremiah 31:31-34; Ezekiel 36:25-27; Luke 22:20; Hebrews 8-10

**Time Period:** Prophesied ~600 BC (Jeremiah); inaugurated at Jesus' death (~AD 30)

**Key Elements - God promises:**
1. **Forgiveness:** Sins forgiven and forgotten
2. **Internal Law:** Law written on hearts, not stone tablets
3. **Intimate Knowledge:** All will know the Lord personally
4. **Holy Spirit:** God's Spirit indwells believers
5. **New Heart:** Heart of stone replaced with heart of flesh
6. **Direct Access:** No need for human mediator

**Type:** Unconditional - based on Christ's finished work, not our performance

**Sign:** The Lord's Supper/Communion (Luke 22:20; 1 Corinthians 11:25)

**Scope:** Universal - available to all who believe (Jew and Gentile)

**Mediator:** Jesus Christ (Hebrews 8:6; 9:15; 12:24)

**Basis:** Blood of Jesus Christ shed on the cross

**Significance:**
- Fulfillment and replacement of Old Covenant
- Achieves what the law could not - internal transformation
- Provides permanent forgiveness through Christ's once-for-all sacrifice
- Creates one new people of God (the church)
- Inaugurated by Christ's death; fully consummated at His return

**Key Old Testament Prophecies:**

**Jeremiah 31:31-34:**
"'The days are coming,' declares the LORD, 'when I will make a new covenant with the people of Israel and with the people of Judah. It will not be like the covenant I made with their ancestors... I will put my law in their minds and write it on their hearts. I will be their God, and they will be my people... They will all know me, from the least of them to the greatest,' declares the LORD. 'For I will forgive their wickedness and will remember their sins no more.'"

**Ezekiel 36:26-27:**
"I will give you a new heart and put a new spirit in you; I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh. And I will put my Spirit in you and move you to follow my decrees and be careful to keep my laws."

**New Testament Fulfillment:**

- **Luke 22:20** - "This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you"
- **2 Corinthians 3:6** - "He has made us competent as ministers of a new covenant—not of the letter but of the Spirit; for the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life"
- **Hebrews 8:6** - "But in fact the ministry Jesus has received is as superior to theirs as the covenant of which he is mediator is superior to the old one"
- **Hebrews 9:15** - "Christ is the mediator of a new covenant"
- **Hebrews 10:16-17** - Quotes Jeremiah 31, declaring it fulfilled

**Superior to the Old Covenant:**
1. **Better Mediator:** Christ vs. Moses
2. **Better Sacrifice:** Christ's blood vs. animal blood
3. **Better Access:** Directly to God vs. through human priests
4. **Better Forgiveness:** Complete and final vs. repeated sacrifices
5. **Better Transformation:** Internal heart change vs. external rules
6. **Better Promise:** Eternal life vs. temporal blessings

**Application:**
- You have direct access to God through Christ - use it!
- Your sins are forgiven completely and finally
- The Holy Spirit indwells you - He empowers obedience
- You don't earn God's favor - Christ earned it for you
- Obedience flows from the heart, not external pressure
- Communion reminds you of the New Covenant sealed in Christ's blood

## How the Covenants Relate

### Progressive Revelation

Each covenant builds on the previous ones, progressively revealing God's redemptive plan:

1. **Edenic:** Sin problem introduced; Redeemer promised
2. **Noahic:** God preserves creation for redemption
3. **Abrahamic:** One family chosen to bless all families
4. **Mosaic:** Law reveals sin; sacrificial system points to ultimate sacrifice
5. **Davidic:** Messiah will come from David's line as eternal King
6. **New:** Christ fulfills all previous covenants

### Christ in Every Covenant

**All covenants point to Jesus Christ:**

- **Edenic:** Jesus is the last Adam who obeyed perfectly (Romans 5:12-19; 1 Corinthians 15:45)
- **Noahic:** Jesus saves through judgment (1 Peter 3:20-21)
- **Abrahamic:** Jesus is the "seed" through whom all nations blessed (Galatians 3:16)
- **Mosaic:** Jesus fulfilled the law and is the final sacrifice (Matthew 5:17; Hebrews 10:10)
- **Davidic:** Jesus is the eternal King on David's throne (Luke 1:32-33)
- **New:** Jesus is the mediator and His blood ratifies it (Hebrews 9:15)

### Continuity and Discontinuity

**Continuity (What Continues):**
- God's faithfulness
- Salvation by grace through faith
- The promise of redemption
- God's desire for relationship with His people

**Discontinuity (What Changes):**
- Old Covenant ceremonial laws don't apply to Christians
- Temple worship replaced by Jesus as the temple
- Levitical priesthood replaced by Jesus our High Priest
- Animal sacrifices replaced by Christ's once-for-all sacrifice
- External law replaced by internal transformation
- National Israel expanded to include Gentiles (the church)

## Theological Implications

### Covenant Theology

**Big Picture:** All of Scripture is understood through the lens of God's covenants. The covenant of works (Edenic) is replaced by the covenant of grace (ultimately the New Covenant in Christ).

**Key Point:** Salvation has always been by grace through faith. The Old Testament saints looked forward to the Messiah; we look back to Him.

### Dispensationalism

**Big Picture:** God relates to humanity through distinct dispensations (ages), with different covenants characterizing different periods.

**Key Point:** While God's methods change, His plan of redemption is consistent.

### Both Agree On:
- Christ is the center of all covenants
- Salvation is by grace through faith (always)
- God is faithful to His promises
- The New Covenant is superior and final

## Practical Applications

### For Your Walk with God

1. **Remember God's Faithfulness:** He kept every covenant promise. Trust Him with your life.

2. **Understand Grace:** You're not under Mosaic Law trying to earn salvation. You're under the New Covenant of grace.

3. **Appreciate Christ's Work:** See how all of Scripture points to Jesus. Everything finds its fulfillment in Him.

4. **Live from the Heart:** God writes His law on your heart now. Obedience flows from love and the Spirit's power, not external pressure.

5. **Celebrate Communion:** The Lord's Supper isn't just a ritual - it's the sign of the New Covenant, reminding you of Christ's blood shed for you.

6. **Know Your Identity:** You're Abraham's spiritual descendant, part of a kingdom of priests, indwelt by God's Spirit.

## Study Suggestions

1. **Trace one covenant** through the entire Bible, noting every reference

2. **Study the covenants chronologically**, seeing how God progressively reveals His plan

3. **Focus on Christ in each covenant** - How does each point to Jesus?

4. **Compare Old and New** - Read Hebrews 8-10 carefully

5. **Memorize key covenant passages:**
   - Genesis 12:1-3 (Abrahamic)
   - Exodus 19:5-6 (Mosaic)
   - Jeremiah 31:31-34 (New)
   - 2 Samuel 7:12-16 (Davidic)
