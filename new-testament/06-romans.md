# Romans

## Book Overview

**Author:** Paul the Apostle
**Date of Writing:** Approximately 57 AD
**Historical Context:** Written from Corinth before Paul's visit to Rome; systematic presentation of the gospel
**Original Audience:** Christians in Rome (both Jewish and Gentile believers)
**Purpose:** To explain the gospel systematically and address Jewish-Gentile tensions in preparation for Paul's visit
**Genre:** Epistle; theological treatise

## One-Sentence Summary

God's righteousness is revealed through the gospel: all humanity stands condemned by sin, but justification comes by faith alone in Christ, empowering believers to live holy lives by the Spirit.

## Book Structure

**I. Introduction (1:1-17)**
- Greetings and Paul's desire to visit Rome
- Theme: The gospel is God's power for salvation

**II. Doctrine: God's Righteousness Revealed (1:18-11:36)**
- *Condemnation:* Universal sin and guilt (1:18-3:20)
- *Justification:* Righteousness through faith in Christ (3:21-5:21)
- *Sanctification:* New life in the Spirit (6:1-8:39)
- *Vindication:* God's plan for Israel (9:1-11:36)

**III. Practice: Righteousness Applied (12:1-15:13)**
- Living sacrifice and renewed mind (12:1-2)
- Service in the body of Christ (12:3-8)
- Love in action (12:9-21)
- Submission to authorities (13:1-7)
- Love fulfills the law (13:8-14)
- Accept the weak in faith (14:1-15:13)

**IV. Conclusion (15:14-16:27)**
- Paul's ministry and travel plans
- Personal greetings and final exhortations

## Key Verses

**Romans 1:16-17** - "For I am not ashamed of the gospel, because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes: first to the Jew, then to the Gentile. For in the gospel the righteousness of God is revealed—a righteousness that is by faith from first to last."
- *Significance:* The thesis statement of Romans, declaring that the gospel reveals God's righteousness through faith, establishing the foundation for the entire letter's argument about justification.

**Romans 3:23-24** - "For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, and all are justified freely by his grace through the redemption that came by Christ Jesus."
- *Significance:* Summarizes humanity's universal problem (sin) and God's universal solution (grace through Christ), the cornerstone of Protestant theology and the doctrine of justification by faith alone.

**Romans 8:1** - "Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."
- *Significance:* The triumphant conclusion to Paul's theological argument, assuring believers of complete freedom from guilt and judgment through union with Christ.

## Key Themes & Messages

### Major Themes

1. **The Gospel of Righteousness**
   - All humanity sinful and under God's wrath
   - Righteousness from God through faith in Jesus Christ
   - Justification by faith alone, apart from works
   - Gospel is "the power of God for salvation"

2. **Universal Sin and Universal Salvation**
   - Jews and Gentiles equally sinful
   - All fall short of God's glory
   - Salvation available to all who believe
   - No distinction - same Lord over all

3. **Justification by Faith**
   - Central doctrine: declared righteous through faith
   - Abraham justified by faith, not works
   - Christ's righteousness credited to believers
   - Foundation of Protestant Reformation

4. **Life in the Spirit**
   - Freedom from sin and death through Christ
   - Spirit produces sanctification and assurance
   - Groaning for full redemption
   - Spirit helps in weakness and intercedes

5. **God's Sovereignty and Israel's Future**
   - God's purposes for Israel not abandoned
   - Israel's temporary hardening allows Gentile inclusion
   - All Israel will be saved
   - God's gifts and calling irrevocable

### Central Message

Romans presents the most systematic explanation of the gospel in Scripture. All humanity stands guilty before God, but God provides righteousness as a gift through faith in Jesus Christ. Justification by faith alone, apart from works, is available to all who believe. The Spirit empowers new life, producing holiness and assurance. Though Israel currently rejects the gospel, God's purposes include both Israel's restoration and Gentile inclusion. The letter calls believers to offer themselves as living sacrifices, transformed by grace to live in love and unity.

## Practical Application

**For Daily Living:**
- **Rest in Grace**: When you fail, remember that righteousness is a gift through faith, not earned by performance - let this free you from spiritual anxiety and self-condemnation
- **Present Your Body**: Make daily choices (what you watch, eat, say, do) as acts of worship, offering yourself as a living sacrifice to God (Romans 12:1)
- **Transform Your Mind**: Actively replace worldly thinking patterns with biblical truth through Scripture reading, prayer, and Christian community
- **Love in Action**: Practice the practical love described in Romans 12-13 - bless those who curse you, share with those in need, live at peace with everyone
- **Accept Others**: Welcome believers whose convictions differ from yours on disputable matters, just as Christ welcomed you despite your weakness

**For Spiritual Growth:**
- Study Romans systematically to build a solid theological foundation - understanding justification, sanctification, and glorification deepens your faith
- Meditate on Romans 8 regularly, letting the Spirit's assurance combat doubt and fear in your Christian walk
- Practice seeing God's sovereignty in difficult circumstances, trusting He works all things for good for those who love Him
- Examine areas where you're still trusting in your own righteousness rather than Christ's, and consciously surrender them to God

## Study Questions

### Introduction and Theme (Romans 1:1-17)

1. **How does Paul describe the gospel in Romans 1:1-4?** What three key facts about Jesus does he emphasize?

2. **Romans 1:16-17 is the thesis statement of the book.** Why is Paul not ashamed of the gospel? What does "the righteousness of God is revealed" mean?

3. **What does "from faith for faith" (1:17) or "by faith from first to last" mean?** How does this relate to Habakkuk 2:4?

---

### Universal Sin and Condemnation (Romans 1:18-3:20)

4. **According to Romans 1:18-20, what do all people know about God?** Why are people "without excuse"?

5. **Trace the downward spiral in Romans 1:21-32.** What pattern do you see? (Suppress truth → futile thinking → darkened hearts → idolatry → God gives them over → further sin)

6. **Romans 2:1-4 warns against judging others while doing the same things.** How does Paul address religious hypocrisy? What is the purpose of God's kindness?

7. **What does Romans 2:12-16 teach about Gentiles and the law?** Can anyone be justified by keeping the law?

8. **Romans 2:17-29 addresses Jewish pride.** What's the difference between outward religion and inward transformation? What does it mean to be a "real Jew"?

9. **Romans 3:9-18 compiles Old Testament verses about human sinfulness.** Summarize the human condition in your own words based on these verses.

10. **What is the purpose of the law according to Romans 3:19-20?** If the law can't save, why did God give it?

---

### Justification by Faith (Romans 3:21-5:21)

11. **Romans 3:21-26 is one of the most important passages in Scripture.** Identify and explain the key terms:
    - Righteousness of God (v. 21-22)
    - Justified (v. 24)
    - Redemption (v. 24)
    - Propitiation/sacrifice of atonement (v. 25)

12. **According to Romans 3:27-31, what is the role of faith vs. works?** How does this affect boasting? Does faith nullify the law?

13. **Why does Paul use Abraham as an example in Romans 4?** What was credited to Abraham as righteousness (v. 3, 22)? When was he justified - before or after circumcision?

14. **Romans 4:23-25 applies Abraham's faith to us.** What must we believe to be justified?

15. **List the blessings of justification described in Romans 5:1-11.** (Peace with God, access to grace, hope, character, love, Holy Spirit, reconciliation, saved from wrath)

16. **Romans 5:12-21 compares Adam and Christ.** Create a chart contrasting what came through Adam vs. what came through Christ.

| Through Adam | Through Christ |
|--------------|----------------|
| Sin entered | Righteousness |
| Death | Life |
| Condemnation | Justification |
| Disobedience | Obedience |

---

### Sanctification: Dead to Sin, Alive in Christ (Romans 6:1-8:39)

17. **What is the question Paul anticipates in Romans 6:1?** ("Shall we go on sinning so grace may increase?") What's his answer and reasoning?

18. **Explain the significance of baptism in Romans 6:3-4.** What does it symbolize about the believer's union with Christ?

19. **Romans 6:11-14 contains imperatives (commands).** What are believers commanded to do? What are we commanded NOT to do?

20. **Romans 6:15-23 discusses slavery.** What are the two types of slavery? What does each lead to? What are the "wages of sin" vs. the "gift of God"?

21. **Romans 7:7-25 is one of the most debated passages.** Who is the "I" in this passage? (Paul before conversion? After? Every Christian?) Describe the internal struggle Paul describes.

22. **What is the answer to the struggle of Romans 7?** (Read Romans 8:1-4) How does the Spirit free us from the law of sin and death?

23. **According to Romans 8:5-17, contrast life in the flesh vs. life in the Spirit.**

| Flesh | Spirit |
|-------|--------|
| Hostile to God | Controlled by Spirit |
| Cannot please God | Leads to life and peace |
| Leads to death | Brings life |

24. **Romans 8:18-27 discusses suffering and hope.** What are we groaning for? Who else is groaning? How does the Spirit help us?

25. **Romans 8:28-30 describes the "golden chain of redemption."** What are the five links? (Foreknew, predestined, called, justified, glorified)

26. **Romans 8:31-39 is one of the most comforting passages in Scripture.** List all the things that CANNOT separate us from God's love. Memorize verses 38-39.

---

### God's Sovereignty and Israel's Future (Romans 9:1-11:36)

27. **Why is Paul anguished in Romans 9:1-5?** What privileges did Israel have?

28. **Romans 9:6-13 discusses God's sovereign choice.** What point is Paul making about Isaac/Ishmael and Jacob/Esau? Does "Jacob I loved, Esau I hated" mean God arbitrarily rejects people?

29. **How does Paul address the objection "Is God unjust?" in Romans 9:14-18?** What's the difference between God hardening Pharaoh and Pharaoh hardening his own heart?

30. **Romans 9:19-29 uses the potter/clay analogy.** What is Paul's point about God's sovereign rights?

31. **According to Romans 9:30-10:4, why did Israel fail to attain righteousness?** What is "the righteousness that is by law" vs. "the righteousness that is by faith"?

32. **Romans 10:9-13 explains how to be saved.** What must one confess and believe? Is salvation available to all people?

33. **Romans 10:14-17 traces the logic of evangelism.** Fill in the blanks: "How can they call on him they have not ______? How can they believe in him they have not ______? How can they hear without a ______?"

34. **Did Israel not hear the gospel? (Romans 10:18-21)** What was Israel's problem? (Knowledge or obedience?)

35. **Has God rejected Israel? (Romans 11:1-10)** What is the "remnant chosen by grace"? What's the difference between remnant and the rest?

36. **Romans 11:11-24 uses the olive tree metaphor.** Who are the natural branches? Who are the wild branches grafted in? What's Paul's warning to Gentile believers?

37. **What is the "mystery" revealed in Romans 11:25-27?** What does "all Israel will be saved" mean? (All individual Jews? Corporate Israel? Jewish remnant throughout history?)

38. **Romans 11:33-36 is a doxology (hymn of praise) responding to God's wisdom.** What attributes of God does Paul celebrate?

---

### Practical Application: Living Out the Gospel (Romans 12:1-15:13)

39. **Romans 12:1-2 is the hinge between doctrine (chapters 1-11) and practice (chapters 12-16).** What does it mean to offer your body as a "living sacrifice"? What does "renewing of your mind" involve?

40. **Romans 12:3-8 discusses spiritual gifts.** List the gifts mentioned. How should you use your gifts in the church?

41. **Romans 12:9-21 gives rapid-fire commands about love.** Choose five that are most challenging for you. How will you practice them this week?

42. **What does Romans 13:1-7 teach about government?** What are Christians' responsibilities toward authorities? Are there limits to submission? (See Acts 5:29)

43. **According to Romans 13:8-10, how does love fulfill the law?** What does it mean to "owe no one anything, except to love"?

44. **Romans 13:11-14 emphasizes urgency.** Why should we "wake up"? What does it mean to "clothe yourselves with the Lord Jesus Christ"?

45. **Romans 14:1-15:13 addresses "disputable matters" (issues where Scripture doesn't give clear commands).** What are Paul's principles for navigating these issues?
    - Don't judge or look down on others (14:3-4)
    - Be convinced in your own mind (14:5)
    - Don't cause others to stumble (14:13)
    - Accept one another (15:7)

46. **What does it mean to be "fully convinced in your own mind" (Romans 14:5)?** How does this apply to issues like diet, holidays, alcohol, entertainment, etc.?

47. **According to Romans 14:17, what is the kingdom of God?** (Not eating/drinking, but righteousness, peace, joy in Holy Spirit)

48. **What is the principle in Romans 14:23?** "Everything that does not come from faith is sin" - what does this mean practically?

---

### Conclusion and Personal Greetings (Romans 15:14-16:27)

49. **What was Paul's mission according to Romans 15:14-22?** Why did he want to visit Rome?

50. **Romans 16 contains many personal greetings.** What can we learn about Paul's relationships and the early church from this chapter?

---

### Theological Reflection Questions

51. **How does Romans answer the question: "How can sinful people be acceptable to a holy God?"**

52. **What is the relationship between justification (being declared righteous) and sanctification (becoming righteous in practice)?** Can you have one without the other?

53. **How does Romans balance God's sovereignty and human responsibility?** Give examples from the text.

54. **How would you explain "justification by faith alone" to someone who thinks they must earn God's acceptance?** Use Romans to support your explanation.

55. **Romans 8:28 is often quoted: "God works all things together for good."** What does this verse actually promise? What does it NOT promise?

56. **How does Romans address the question "Can I lose my salvation?"** What assurances does it give?

57. **What role does the Holy Spirit play in the Christian life according to Romans?**
    - Romans 5:5
    - Romans 8:2, 9-11, 13-16, 26-27
    - Romans 15:13, 16

58. **How should Romans 9-11 shape Christian attitudes toward Jewish people?** What should we avoid? What should we embrace?

The Letter to the Romans (Pillar New Testament Commentary) by Douglas J. Moo - Widely regarded as the best contemporary commentary on Romans, clear and theologically rich.

**For Beginners**
Romans (Tyndale New Testament Commentaries) by John Stott - Accessible, clear, and pastoral exposition from one of evangelicalism's finest Bible teachers.

**For In-Depth Study**
Romans (NIV Application Commentary) by Douglas J. Moo - Excellent at connecting Paul's theology to contemporary life with robust exegesis and practical application.

**Technical/Academic**
The Epistle to the Romans (NIGTC) by Douglas J. Moo - Comprehensive scholarly treatment with extensive interaction with Greek text and interpretive debates.
