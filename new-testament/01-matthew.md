# Matthew

## Book Overview

**Author:** Matthew (Levi), former tax collector and apostle
**Date of Writing:** Approximately 50-70 AD
**Historical Context:** Written to Jewish Christians, likely before Jerusalem's destruction (70 AD)
**Original Audience:** Jewish believers and seekers
**Purpose:** To prove Jesus is the promised Messiah and King, fulfilling Old Testament prophecy
**Genre:** Gospel; theological biography

## One-Sentence Summary

Jesus is the promised Messiah-King who fulfills Old Testament prophecy and establishes God's kingdom through His authoritative teaching, sacrificial death, and victorious resurrection.

## Book Structure

**I. The Coming of the King (1-4)**
- Birth narratives and genealogy (1-2)
- John the Baptist and Jesus' baptism (3)
- Temptation and beginning of ministry (4)

**II. The Sermon on the Mount (5-7)**
- Kingdom ethics and righteousness

**III. The Authority of the King (8-10)**
- Ten miracles demonstrating power (8-9)
- Commissioning of the twelve apostles (10)

**IV. Responses to the King (11-13)**
- Growing opposition and rejection (11-12)
- Kingdom parables (13)

**V. The Training of Disciples (14-18)**
- Jesus withdraws to train the twelve (14-17)
- Church life and discipline (18)

**VI. The Journey to Jerusalem (19-25)**
- Teaching on the way to Jerusalem (19-20)
- Triumphal entry and temple confrontations (21-23)
- Olivet Discourse on end times (24-25)

**VII. The Passion and Resurrection (26-28)**
- Betrayal, trial, crucifixion (26-27)
- Resurrection and Great Commission (28)

## Key Verses

**Matthew 5:17** - *"Do not think that I have come to abolish the Law or the Prophets; I have not come to abolish them but to fulfill them."*
- **Significance:** Establishes Jesus as the fulfillment of all Old Testament expectations, the central theme of Matthew's Gospel.

**Matthew 16:16-18** - *"Simon Peter answered, 'You are the Messiah, the Son of the living God.' Jesus replied, 'Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah, for this was not revealed to you by flesh and blood, but by my Father in heaven. And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church.'"*
- **Significance:** Peter's confession marks the climax of Jesus' identity revelation and introduces the church that will carry His mission forward.

**Matthew 28:18-20** - *"Then Jesus came to them and said, 'All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and teaching them to obey everything I have commanded you. And surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age.'"*
- **Significance:** The Great Commission extends Jesus' messianic authority to all nations and defines the church's ongoing mission.

## Key Themes & Messages

### Major Themes

1. **Jesus as Messiah-King**
   - Genealogy traces Jesus to Abraham and David
   - Fulfillment of Old Testament prophecies emphasized throughout
   - Kingdom of heaven is central message
   - Jesus is the promised King of Israel

2. **Fulfillment of the Old Testament**
   - Repeated phrase: "to fulfill what was spoken through the prophet"
   - Jesus fulfills the Law and Prophets
   - New covenant completes and surpasses old covenant
   - Bridge between Old and New Testaments

3. **The Kingdom of Heaven**
   - Jesus announces kingdom has come near
   - Sermon on the Mount describes kingdom ethics
   - Parables reveal kingdom mysteries
   - Already present yet still coming in fullness

4. **Discipleship and Mission**
   - Call to follow Jesus and learn from Him
   - Great Commission: make disciples of all nations
   - Cost of discipleship clearly stated
   - Commission extends beyond Israel to all peoples

5. **Teaching of Jesus**
   - Five major teaching discourses (like five books of Moses)
   - Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5-7)
   - Emphasis on Jesus as authoritative teacher
   - Higher righteousness than scribes and Pharisees

### Central Message

Matthew presents Jesus as the long-awaited Messiah-King who fulfills Old Testament prophecy and establishes God's kingdom. Jesus' teaching, particularly the Sermon on the Mount, reveals kingdom ethics that surpass external law-keeping. Though rejected by Israel's leaders, Jesus accomplishes redemption through His death and resurrection, commissioning disciples to extend His kingdom to all nations. The Gospel emphasizes Jesus' authority as King, Teacher, and Lord.

## Practical Application

**For Daily Living:**
- **Live Kingdom Ethics**: Apply the Sermon on the Mount practically—be a peacemaker, show mercy, let your yes be yes, and go the extra mile in relationships
- **Pursue Heart Righteousness**: Move beyond rule-keeping to examining your heart motives in anger, lust, honesty, and love
- **Practice Sacrificial Giving**: Give, pray, and fast in secret for God's eyes alone, not for public recognition or approval
- **Seek First God's Kingdom**: Make daily decisions based on kingdom priorities rather than anxiety about material needs (Matthew 6:33)
- **Make Disciples, Not Just Converts**: Invest in others by teaching them to obey all Jesus commanded, not just intellectual knowledge

**For Spiritual Growth:**
- Memorize and meditate on Jesus' teachings in the Sermon on the Mount, asking the Spirit to transform your character to match kingdom values
- Study how Jesus fulfills Old Testament prophecy to deepen your confidence in God's faithfulness and the unity of Scripture
- Embrace Jesus' authority as King over every area of your life—relationships, finances, career, time, and priorities
- Participate actively in the Great Commission by sharing your faith and discipling others in your sphere of influence

## Study Questions

### Understanding the Book
1. How does Matthew's genealogy of Jesus (1:1-17) demonstrate that He is the rightful heir to David's throne and the fulfillment of God's promises to Abraham?
2. What is the significance of the title "Messiah-King" throughout Matthew's Gospel? How does Jesus both fulfill and transform expectations of the Messiah?
3. How does the phrase "to fulfill what was spoken through the prophet" function in Matthew? List at least 5 Old Testament prophecies Matthew shows Jesus fulfilling.
4. What is the "kingdom of heaven"? How is it both "already" (present) and "not yet" (future) in Matthew's Gospel?
5. How do the five major teaching discourses in Matthew parallel the five books of Moses (the Pentateuch)?

### Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5-7)
6. What do the Beatitudes (5:3-12) teach about the character of kingdom citizens? How do they contrast with worldly values?
7. How does Jesus' teaching "You have heard it said... but I say to you" (5:21-48) demonstrate the relationship between the Old and New Covenants?
8. What does Jesus teach about authentic versus hypocritical religion in areas like giving, prayer, and fasting (6:1-18)?
9. How does Matthew 6:33 ("seek first the kingdom of God and His righteousness") summarize kingdom priorities?
10. What is the difference between the wise and foolish builders (7:24-27)? How does this apply to your response to Jesus' teaching?

### Jesus' Ministry and Miracles
11. What do Jesus' miracles reveal about His identity and the nature of God's kingdom? Give specific examples.
12. How do Jesus' interactions with Gentiles (e.g., centurion in 8:5-13, Canaanite woman in 15:21-28) foreshadow the gospel going to all nations?
13. Why does Jesus often tell people not to tell others about their healings (e.g., 8:4; 9:30)? What does this reveal about His mission?

### Parables and Teaching
14. What is the purpose of Jesus teaching in parables according to Matthew 13:10-17? What does this reveal about human hearts?
15. Choose three parables from Matthew 13 and explain what each teaches about the kingdom of heaven.
16. What does the parable of the unforgiving servant (18:21-35) teach about forgiveness? How should this impact your relationships?

### Opposition and Conflict
17. How does opposition to Jesus develop throughout Matthew's Gospel? What are the main accusations against Him?
18. What is the "blasphemy against the Holy Spirit" (12:31-32)? Why is it unforgivable?
19. How does Jesus respond to the Pharisees' emphasis on external law-keeping versus heart transformation? (See chapters 15, 23)

### Discipleship
20. What does Jesus mean by "take up your cross and follow me" (16:24)? What does this cost of discipleship look like today?
21. How does Peter's confession (16:16) and subsequent rebuke (16:21-23) illustrate the journey of discipleship?
22. What characteristics should mark Jesus' disciples according to Matthew? (Consider humility, servanthood, forgiveness, mission)
23. What is the relationship between faith and doubt in Matthew's Gospel? (See 14:31; 17:20; 21:21; 28:17)

### The Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24-25)
24. What signs does Jesus give of the end times? How should believers respond to these signs?
25. What do the parables of the ten virgins (25:1-13) and the talents (25:14-30) teach about readiness for Christ's return?
26. How does the sheep and goats judgment (25:31-46) relate to genuine faith? What is the connection between loving Jesus and caring for "the least of these"?

### Passion and Resurrection
27. How does Jesus demonstrate His willing obedience to the Father in Gethsemane (26:36-46)?
28. What Old Testament passages and themes does Matthew highlight in the crucifixion narrative?
29. What is the significance of the temple veil tearing in two (27:51)? What access does this provide?
30. How does the Great Commission (28:18-20) summarize Jesus' authority and the mission of the church?

### Personal Application
31. Which of Jesus' teachings in Matthew challenges you most? How will you respond?
32. In what ways do you tend toward being like the Pharisees (focusing on external religion rather than heart transformation)?
33. What does it practically mean for you to "seek first the kingdom of God" (6:33) in your current life circumstances?
34. How is God calling you to participate in the Great Commission in your sphere of influence?
35. What aspect of Jesus' character in Matthew draws you to worship Him? How will this change how you live?

### Discussion Questions for Groups
36. How would you explain to a Jewish friend that Jesus is the promised Messiah based on Matthew's Gospel?
37. What is the relationship between faith and works in Matthew? How do you avoid both legalism and cheap grace?
38. Discuss the balance between Jesus' teachings on judgment/hell and His compassion for sinners. How should this shape our evangelism?
39. How does the church today fulfill (or fail to fulfill) Jesus' vision for His community as seen in Matthew 18?
40. In what ways can we apply Jesus' counter-cultural Sermon on the Mount ethics in our contemporary society?

## Recommended Resources

### Commentaries

**Best Overall**
The Gospel According to Matthew (NICNT) by R.T. France - Combines scholarly depth with pastoral insight, excellent on Jesus' fulfillment of Old Testament prophecy.

**For Beginners**
Matthew (Tyndale New Testament Commentaries) by R.T. France - Clear, concise, and accessible introduction to Matthew's Gospel with practical applications.

**For In-Depth Study**
Matthew (NIV Application Commentary) by Michael J. Wilkins - Bridges the gap between biblical text and contemporary life with rich exegesis and practical application.

**Technical/Academic**
The Gospel According to Matthew (NIGTC) by D.A. Carson - Comprehensive scholarly treatment with extensive discussion of Greek text and interpretive issues.
